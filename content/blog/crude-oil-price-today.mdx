## What Is Happening

Oil has round-tripped its entire war rally and now cannot lift off the floor. **From a peak above $110 a barrel earlier in 2026, crude slid back below $75, with WTI near $69 and Brent near $72**, sitting close to a four-month low. The supply scare that drove the spike has been replaced by worries about too much supply.

The first driver was the ceasefire. The US and Iran ended the war, Iran reopened the Strait of Hormuz, and tanker traffic through the chokepoint that carries roughly a fifth of the world's oil has normalised, restoring the barrels the wartime blockade had removed. **With supply back, the geopolitical risk premium has drained out of the price.**

The second driver is OPEC+. **The producer group, led by Saudi Arabia and Russia, approved another output increase of about 188,000 barrels per day for next month**, adding supply into a market where demand growth looks uneven. Restored Iranian flows plus rising OPEC+ output have turned the conversation toward a potential glut, which is why crude keeps testing its lows rather than recovering.

## Why This Matters for India

Few countries gain more from cheaper oil than India. **India imports more than 85% of its crude, so a 40% fall in prices directly shrinks the import bill, narrows the trade deficit, and takes pressure off the rupee.** At the peak, expensive oil had been a drag on all three; now that drag has reversed, even if a weak rupee is offsetting part of the gain.

The inflation channel matters just as much. Fuel and transport costs ripple through the prices of almost everything, so lower crude feeds into softer inflation over time, giving the Reserve Bank of India more room on policy. You can see how the currency side is moving on our [rupee vs dollar today](/blog/rupee-dollar-today) page, and how equities are reacting in our [Indian stock market today](/blog/indian-stock-market-today) wrap.

For the market, cheaper oil is a tailwind for oil marketing companies, paint makers, tyre firms, aviation, and others that use oil as a key input, while it pressures upstream producers whose revenue falls with the price.

## What To Watch

The first thing to watch is OPEC+ policy. **Because rising output is now a key driver, the group's next decisions on production will set the tone**, since a pause or reversal of the increases could put a floor under prices.

The second is whether the ceasefire holds. The crash began when the war ended, so any breakdown in the framework or fresh disruption to Hormuz could send prices spiking again, just as the conflict did earlier this year.

The third is global demand. Weaker growth in major economies could keep a lid on oil even with the geopolitical drama gone, so the demand outlook becomes a bigger swing factor as the supply picture loosens.

## Risks To Monitor

The clearest risk is a return of conflict. The ceasefire is a framework, not a permanent peace, and the Strait of Hormuz remains the world's most sensitive oil chokepoint.

A second risk is an OPEC+ reversal. If low prices prompt the group to stop adding barrels or cut output to defend prices, the market could tighten again quickly.

The third, for India specifically, is the rupee. If cheaper oil is offset by a weaker rupee near record lows, some of the benefit on the import bill is lost, so the currency and crude need to be watched together. This is general information, not investment advice.

Oil near $69 looks like relief after a brutal year, but the same forces that sank prices, restored supply and a producer group willing to pump, could keep it low for a while yet. For an importer like India, this window of cheap crude is a chance to repair the import bill, for as long as the glut fears last.
