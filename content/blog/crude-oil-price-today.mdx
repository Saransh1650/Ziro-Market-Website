The oil market's calm just shattered. **Crude oil price today is about $73 a barrel for WTI and $76 for Brent as of July 8, 2026, both up roughly 3% in a single session, after the US launched fresh strikes on Iran and reimposed sanctions on Iranian oil in response to attacks on ships near the Strait of Hormuz.** Days after crude tested a four-month low on glut fears, the war premium is back.

For India, which imports most of its oil, the direction of this move is exactly the wrong one. A rising crude price threatens the import bill, the rupee, and the inflation outlook all at once.

## What Is Happening

The escalation was fast and sharp. **After Iran's Revolutionary Guards attacked commercial vessels near the Strait of Hormuz, the US struck more than 80 Iranian targets, hitting air defences, command centres, coastal radar, and dozens of IRGC boats**, and reimposed the oil sanctions it had eased under the interim ceasefire. The Treasury gave buyers of Iranian oil until July 17 to wind down transactions.

Iran responded in kind, launching attacks on US military sites in Kuwait and Bahrain, which deepened fears that the conflict is widening rather than ending. **The Strait of Hormuz, which carries roughly a fifth of the world's oil, is once again the market's biggest worry**, since any disruption there can choke global supply overnight.

The price reaction was immediate. WTI jumped about 3.1% to $72.61 and Brent about 3.1% to $76.49, unwinding the slide that had taken crude toward a four-month low only days earlier on worries about an OPEC+ supply glut. The glut story has not vanished, but for now the geopolitical risk premium is back in charge.

## Why This Matters for India

Few countries lose more from costlier oil than India. **India imports more than 85% of its crude, so every dollar on the oil price widens the import bill, pressures the trade deficit, and weighs on the rupee**, the exact reverse of the windfall the country enjoyed when crude was sliding.

The inflation channel is the bigger worry. Fuel and transport costs ripple through the prices of almost everything, so a sustained rise in crude feeds into higher inflation over time, which can limit the Reserve Bank of India's room on policy. You can see how the currency side is moving on our [rupee vs dollar today](/blog/rupee-dollar-today) page, and how equities reacted in our [Indian stock market today](/blog/indian-stock-market-today) wrap, where oil and gas and FMCG stocks led the fall.

For the market, costlier oil pressures paint makers, tyre firms, aviation, and other heavy oil users, while it helps upstream producers whose revenue rises with the price. The swing from cheap to dear crude reshuffles which sectors win and lose almost overnight.

## What To Watch

The first thing to watch is the Strait of Hormuz itself. **Because the rally is built on the threat to shipping, any actual blockage or further tanker attacks could send prices spiking well beyond current levels**, while signs of de-escalation could just as quickly pull them back.

The second is the sanctions timeline. With buyers of Iranian oil given until July 17 to wind down, the market will watch how much supply actually comes off, and whether other producers step in to fill the gap.

The third is OPEC+. The group had been adding barrels into an oversupplied market, so how it responds to both the price spike and the supply threat will shape where crude settles once the immediate shock passes.

## Risks To Monitor

The clearest risk is a genuine disruption to Hormuz. A prolonged closure would be a supply shock of a different order, capable of pushing oil sharply higher and hitting import-dependent economies like India hard.

A second risk is a wider regional war. Iran's strikes on Kuwait and Bahrain raise the danger of the conflict spreading, which would keep a large risk premium in the oil price.

The third, for India specifically, is the double hit of costlier oil and a weaker rupee, which together can inflate the landed cost of fuel and feed straight into inflation. This is general information, not investment advice.

Oil back near $76 is a reminder of how fast the story can flip. A week ago the market feared too much supply; today it fears too little. For an importer like India, the return of the war premium turns a tailwind into a headwind, and the Strait of Hormuz is once again the single chart that matters most.
