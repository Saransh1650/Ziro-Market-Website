The oil market has gone from crisis to calm. **Crude oil price today is about $70 a barrel for WTI and $74 for Brent as of August 2026, well below the July peak above $85, as easing Middle East tensions and steady supply pull the war-risk premium out of the price.** After a July dominated by the Strait of Hormuz standoff, oil is once again trading on supply and demand rather than headlines.

For an oil importer like India, the shift is a relief. The same barrel that threatened the rupee and inflation a month ago is now a tailwind.

## Why has oil fallen?

The war premium has drained away. **The July standoff over the Strait of Hormuz, when Iran declared the waterway closed and the US imposed a blockade, eased as tensions de-escalated and tanker traffic normalised, and the fear premium that had pushed Brent above $85 came out of the price.** With no supply disruption materialising, the market reset to fundamentals.

Supply did the rest. Output from OPEC+ and other producers has been adequate, so with the threat to the world's most important oil chokepoint fading, there was little reason for prices to stay elevated. The result is Brent back in the low $70s, a level set by barrels and demand rather than by headlines.

The July move was violent, which makes the calm notable. When Iran declared the Strait of Hormuz closed and the US imposed a blockade, Brent spiked above $85 within days on fears that a fifth of the world's seaborne oil could be cut off. That the price has fully unwound to the low $70s shows the market judged the disruption unlikely to last, and that physical supply kept flowing throughout. The speed of both the spike and the retreat is a reminder of how much of the oil price is sentiment, not barrels.

## What it means for India

Cheaper oil is one of the clearest positives for India. **Because the country imports more than 85% of its crude, a lower price directly shrinks the import bill, narrows the trade deficit, and eases pressure on the rupee**, which has recovered from its July record low near 96, as our [rupee vs dollar today](/blog/rupee-dollar-today) page tracks. The chain that made high oil so damaging now runs in reverse.

The inflation link matters most. Lower crude feeds through to lower fuel and transport costs, cooling inflation and giving the RBI more room to cut rates later in the year, as our [will the RBI cut rates again in 2026?](/blog/will-rbi-cut-rates-2026) outlook explains. That is why calmer oil has been part of the market's firmer mood, covered in our [Indian stock market today](/blog/indian-stock-market-today) wrap.

The benefit reaches beyond the pump. Because oil is India's single largest import, a lower crude price improves the current account, supports the rupee, and eases the government's subsidy and fiscal maths, all at once. It also helps oil-using sectors, from paints and tyres to airlines and logistics, whose input costs fall with crude. In short, cheap oil is a broad, quiet tailwind for the Indian economy, which is why its direction matters far beyond energy stocks.

## What to watch

The first thing to watch is the Middle East. **Because the recent premium was entirely about geopolitics, any fresh threat to the Strait of Hormuz could put the risk premium straight back into the price**, as our [what happens if the Strait of Hormuz closes](/blog/what-happens-if-strait-of-hormuz-closes) explainer describes.

The second is OPEC+ policy. A decision to cut output to defend prices would tighten the market and lift crude, regardless of the calmer geopolitics.

The third is global demand. A stronger-than-expected pickup in growth, or a slowdown, would move oil in either direction now that the war premium has gone.

## Risks to monitor

The clearest risk is a re-escalation in the Gulf, which could reverse the recent fall quickly given how fast the July spike happened.

A second risk is an OPEC+ supply cut, which would support prices even in a calm geopolitical environment.

The third, for India specifically, is that the benefit is not permanent: oil is volatile, so the current relief on the rupee and inflation could fade if crude climbs again. This is general information, not investment advice.

Brent back near $74 is a market that has exhaled after a tense July, trading on fundamentals instead of fear. For India, it has quietly removed one of the biggest weights on the rupee, inflation, and the RBI's options. The relief is real, but oil has shown how fast a narrow waterway thousands of kilometres away can change the story, so the calm is worth watching as closely as the crisis was.
