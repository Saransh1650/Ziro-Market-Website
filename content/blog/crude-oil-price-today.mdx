Oil has taken a breath, but it has not calmed down. **Crude oil price today is about $71 a barrel for WTI and $76 for Brent as of the last close on July 10, 2026, easing from a mid-week peak above $79 as markets chose to look past the US-Iran conflict, even as a risk premium lingers because shipping through the Strait of Hormuz remains disrupted.** For the week, crude still booked a gain of roughly 4 to 5%.

The pullback is a relief for India, but only a partial one. Prices remain well above where they sat at the start of the month, and the conflict that drove them up is unresolved.

## What Is Happening

The panic has faded, but the fear has not. **After spiking above $79 when the US struck Iran and reimposed oil sanctions, Brent eased back to near $76 as investors decided the ceasefire, though declared over, had not fully collapsed**, with technical talks between Washington and Tehran continuing and a Qatari delegation joining the diplomacy. The market chose relief over dread, at least for now.

Yet the risk premium refuses to disappear. Tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz, which carries roughly a fifth of the world's oil, stayed well below normal as shipowners avoided the conflict zone. **As long as the Strait is disrupted, the threat of a supply shock keeps a floor under prices**, which is why crude eased rather than crashed back to its earlier lows.

The week captured the whole arc: a sharp spike on the strikes, then a partial retreat as calmer heads prevailed, leaving crude up about 4 to 5% for the week. It is a market pricing an uneasy truce rather than either peace or war.

## Why This Matters for India

Even a partial pullback helps India, but the level still stings. **India imports more than 85% of its crude, so Brent near $76, above where it began the month, keeps the import bill elevated and feeds into inflation**, one reason June CPI is expected to rise above the RBI's 4% target. The relief from the peak is real, but the pressure has not fully lifted.

The rupee side improved with the oil pullback. As crude eased, the rupee firmed to about 95.33, covered on our [rupee vs dollar today](/blog/rupee-dollar-today) page, easing one channel of pressure. Our [how crude oil affects the Indian economy](/blog/how-crude-oil-affects-indian-economy) explainer traces how tightly the oil price, the rupee, and inflation are linked.

For the market, the oil pullback helped drive Friday's rally, as covered in our [Indian stock market today](/blog/indian-stock-market-today) wrap. Cheaper crude eases the squeeze on oil-using sectors like aviation and paints, while a still-elevated price supports upstream producers.

## What To Watch

The first thing to watch is the Strait of Hormuz. **Because the risk premium rests on the threat to shipping, any fresh tanker attack or blockage could send prices spiking again**, while a genuine easing of tensions and a return of normal traffic would let crude fall further.

The second is the US-Iran talks. Progress toward a durable agreement would drain the war premium, while a breakdown would revive it, so the diplomacy is now as important as the fighting.

The third is OPEC+ supply. The group had been adding barrels before the crisis, so how it manages output into an uncertain market will shape where crude settles once the geopolitical noise fades.

## Risks To Monitor

The clearest risk is a renewed escalation. The ceasefire is strained and unresolved, so a fresh flare-up could quickly push oil back above $79.

A second risk is a prolonged Hormuz disruption. If shipping stays impaired for weeks, the persistent risk premium could keep crude elevated even without new strikes.

The third, for India specifically, is the combination of firm oil and rising inflation, which together could limit the RBI's room to support growth. This is general information, not investment advice.

Brent near $76 is a market caught between relief and unease. The immediate panic has passed, but with the Strait of Hormuz still disrupted and the conflict unresolved, oil remains one headline away from its next big move, and for an importer like India, that keeps it the chart to watch.
