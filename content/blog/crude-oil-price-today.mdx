The oil shock is not over. **Brent crude fell 0.88% to about $105.7 a barrel on 25 September 2026, after an intraday high just above $108 and a peak of $108.75 in mid-September when Saudi Arabia shut its East-West pipeline.** It is up about 2% on the week, having touched an 11-day low near $101 on Monday. US WTI fell about 2% to $92.72.

For an economy whose crude import dependence crossed 90% in FY26, this is not an energy story. It is an inflation story, a rupee story and an interest rate story arriving together, and the full transmission is set out in our note on [Brent crossing $100](/blog/brent-crude-100-dollars-september-2026).

## Why did Brent spike in mid-September?

Because the Gulf lost its bypass. **Saudi Arabia shut its entire East-West pipeline on 11 September 2026 after Houthi strikes and drone attacks damaged pumping stations; the 1,200 km line carries about 4 to 5 million barrels a day from the eastern fields to the Red Sea port of Yanbu, which is how Saudi crude avoids the Strait of Hormuz.** With Hormuz already disrupted, losing the bypass sent Brent up 2.9% to settle at $108.75, more than 20% higher for the month.

**The war also has a second chokepoint.** Houthi forces seized the Red Sea port of Mocha and then the length of Yemen's Red Sea coastline in September 2026, putting Bab el-Mandeb, the southern gate to the Suez Canal, under the same cloud as Hormuz. India's crude comes in through one and its exports to Europe go out through the other, a squeeze our [two chokepoints](/blog/two-chokepoints-hormuz-bab-el-mandeb-india-2026) piece sets out.

## What is holding it below $108?

Two things are capping the rally. **US and Iranian negotiators are exploring a phased deal that would lift naval blockades on tankers crossing the Strait of Hormuz and ease US sanctions on Iran, and a possible US ban on diesel exports is weighing on US crude**, even as an Iranian military adviser threatened to widen the conflict to the Indian Ocean and Houthi missiles targeted Saudi Arabia on Thursday.

The rally had been built in stages before that. A US authorisation permitting Iranian crude sales expired on 21 August 2026, Operation Economic Outcast sanctions landed on 24 August, and US and Iranian forces traded strikes at sea through early September, taking Brent from $92.06 on 24 August to above $100 on 9 September. Our [Iran sanctions and India](/blog/india-iran-oil-sanctions-august-2026) analysis covers that step.

## What it means for India

The transmission is direct and fast. **Every sustained $10 on the barrel widens India's import bill, costs an estimated 20 to 30 basis points of GDP growth, and pushes up transport and fuel costs that reach food and manufactured goods within a quarter.** The rupee, which fell past 96 to the dollar in mid-September, closed at 95.80 on 25 September.

The inflation link matters for policy. **Indian retail inflation rose to 4.82% in August 2026, a seven-month high, which narrows the Reserve Bank of India's room to cut from its 5.25% repo rate ahead of the 7 October decision**, as our [will the RBI cut rates again in 2026](/blog/will-rbi-cut-rates-2026) outlook sets out. Equities feel it directly: the Nifty 50 fell 1.64% to 23,063.10 on 24 September as crude and US yields rose, then rebounded 0.34% to 23,140.50 on 25 September, as our [Indian stock market today](/blog/indian-stock-market-today) wrap covers.

There is a sharper edge for India specifically. **President Trump signed the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act on 18 September, authorising tariffs of up to 100% on the five largest buyers of Russian energy and directing action within 30 days.** India imported about 2.08 million barrels a day of Russian crude in August 2026, roughly 45% of its total, which places it squarely inside the law's scope.

## What to watch

**The first is whether the US-Iran talks in New York produce a signed phased reopening of Hormuz**, because talk of diplomacy has already kept several dollars out of the price and a collapse would put them back.

**The second is the Saudi East-West pipeline.** A full, confirmed restart would remove the specific premium added on 11 September; further strikes on pumping stations would restore it.

**The third is the 30-day window under the new sanctions law**, which runs to mid-October. Applying tariffs to India or China would be an inflation event in its own right, separate from anything at Hormuz, as our [Russia sanctions and India](/blog/russia-sanctions-act-2026-india-tariff-risk) piece explains.

## Risks to monitor

<Callout type="risk" title="The combination India cannot hedge">

Expensive oil and a hawkish US Federal Reserve are the same trade hitting the rupee from two directions. Oil widens the trade deficit while high US yields pull capital out, and the Reserve Bank of India cannot ease into that without risking the currency.

</Callout>

The second risk runs the other way. Oil above $105 still prices a lot of bad news, so a signed de-escalation could take another ten dollars out quickly, as the July 2026 episode showed.

The third is OPEC+. Higher prices give the group room to raise output, which would cap the rally, while holding output steady into a supply squeeze would extend it. This is general information, not investment advice.

Brent above $105 has taken back the room India briefly had on Monday, and a law signed the week before could take more. What happens next will show up in tanker manifests, insurance quotes and pipeline repair reports before it shows up in the price of Indian petrol.
