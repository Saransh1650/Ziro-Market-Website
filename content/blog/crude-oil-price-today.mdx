The oil rally is not letting up. **Crude oil price today is about $74 a barrel for WTI and above $79 for Brent as of July 9, 2026, extending a sharp weekly climb as escalating US-Iran strikes and fresh oil sanctions keep the threat to the Strait of Hormuz front of mind.** From near $69 a week ago, Brent has jumped more than $10, and the market that recently feared a glut now fears a shortage.

For India, which imports most of its oil, the direction is punishing. A rising crude price threatens the import bill, the rupee, and the inflation outlook all at once.

## What Is Happening

The escalation keeps feeding the rally. **After Iran's Revolutionary Guards attacked commercial vessels near the Strait of Hormuz, the US struck Iranian targets and revoked the waiver that had allowed Iranian oil sales**, and the conflict has since widened with strikes on both sides. Each new headline adds to the fear that supply through the Strait could be disrupted.

The Strait of Hormuz is the heart of the worry. It carries roughly a fifth of the world's oil, so even the threat of disruption, and the reluctance of shipowners to send tankers through a conflict zone, is enough to lift prices. **Brent has climbed above $79 from near $69 only a week ago**, a move that has erased the earlier narrative of an OPEC+ supply glut.

The reversal has been swift. Days ago the market was worried about too much oil as OPEC+ added barrels; now it is pricing the risk of too little as the Gulf conflict escalates. That whiplash shows how quickly geopolitics can override the supply-and-demand picture.

## Why This Matters for India

Few countries lose more from costlier oil than India. **India imports more than 85% of its crude, so every dollar on the oil price widens the import bill, pressures the trade deficit, and weighs on the rupee**, which has slipped toward 95.6 as crude has climbed. The oil spike was also a key trigger for the roughly 2% crash in Indian equities on July 8.

The inflation channel is the bigger worry. Fuel and transport costs ripple through the prices of almost everything, so a sustained rise in crude feeds into higher inflation over time, which can limit the Reserve Bank of India's room on policy. You can see how the currency side is moving on our [rupee vs dollar today](/blog/rupee-dollar-today) page, and how equities reacted in our [Indian stock market today](/blog/indian-stock-market-today) wrap.

For the market, costlier oil pressures paint makers, tyre firms, aviation, and other heavy oil users, while it helps upstream producers whose revenue rises with the price. Our explainer on [how crude oil affects the Indian economy](/blog/how-crude-oil-affects-indian-economy) traces the full chain from the barrel to the budget.

## What To Watch

The first thing to watch is the Strait of Hormuz. **Because the rally is built on the threat to shipping, any actual blockage or further tanker attacks could send prices spiking well beyond current levels**, while credible de-escalation could pull them back fast.

The second is the sanctions impact. With the US having revoked Iran's oil-sale waiver, the market will watch how much supply actually comes off and whether other producers step in to fill the gap.

The third is OPEC+. The group had been adding barrels into an oversupplied market, so how it responds to both the price spike and the supply threat will shape where crude settles once the immediate shock passes.

## Risks To Monitor

The clearest risk is a genuine disruption to Hormuz. A prolonged closure would be a supply shock of a different order, capable of pushing oil far higher and hitting import-dependent economies like India hard.

A second risk is a wider regional war. The conflict has already spread to strikes on US sites in the Gulf, which raises the danger of a broader war that would keep a large risk premium in oil.

The third, for India specifically, is the double hit of costlier oil and a weaker rupee, which together inflate the landed cost of fuel and feed straight into inflation. This is general information, not investment advice.

Brent above $79 shows how fast the story has flipped. A week ago the market feared too much supply; today it fears too little. For an importer like India, the return of the war premium turns a tailwind into a headwind, and the Strait of Hormuz is once again the single chart that matters most.
