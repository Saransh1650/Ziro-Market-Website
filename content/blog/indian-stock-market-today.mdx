Friday was the better day inside a worse week. **The Nifty 50 closed at 23,346.40 on 18 September 2026, up 75.80 points or 0.33%, while the Sensex finished almost unchanged at 74,294.96, and both still ended the week lower, the sixth straight weekly decline for Indian equities.** Metals and realty led, information technology lagged.

What the headline indices hid was a much stronger session underneath them. **The Nifty Midcap 100 rose 1.24% and the Nifty Smallcap 100 rose 1.74% on 18 September**, a gap that usually shows domestic money working while foreign money sits out.

The external pressure also eased slightly. **Brent crude slipped back below $100 to about $99.29 on 19 September 2026, down from roughly $107 a week earlier**, which takes some weight off the import bill even as a new American sanctions law puts a different kind of risk on India's oil supply.

## What moved the market

Oil moved first, and this time in the helpful direction. **Brent fell from about $107 in mid-September to $99.29 on 19 September 2026, the first close under $100 since the Strait of Hormuz disruption began to bite**, tracked daily on our [crude oil price today](/blog/crude-oil-price-today) page. India imports more than 90% of the crude it burns, so every $10 off the barrel is worth billions of dollars a year to the current account.

The currency steadied with it. **The rupee closed at 95.88 to the dollar on 18 September 2026, marginally stronger than the previous close of 95.94**, after breaching 96 intraday the day before. The running level sits on our [rupee versus dollar today](/blog/rupee-dollar-today) page.

Washington supplied the week's new variable. **President Trump signed the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act into law on 18 September 2026, authorising tariffs of up to 100% on the five largest buyers of Russian energy, a list that includes India.** Nothing has been imposed yet, and the law gives the administration 30 days to act.

## Who is buying and who is selling

The year-long pattern cracked, at least for a day. **Foreign institutional investors bought a net Rs 599.54 crore of Indian equities on 18 September 2026, their first net buying session in weeks, while domestic institutions added Rs 1,019.69 crore.**

The month still reads the same way. **Foreign investors remain net sellers of about Rs 7,041 crore in September 2026, against roughly Rs 36,219 crore of domestic institutional buying**, the gap that has kept the index down 11.5% from its January record rather than far more. ## What comes next

**The National Stock Exchange offer for sale closes on 21 September 2026 and lists on the BSE on 24 September, at Rs 22,562 crore the largest IPO India has ever run.** It was subscribed about 1.16 times by the end of day two and the grey market premium had cooled to roughly Rs 70 over the Rs 1,785 upper band by 19 September, from about Rs 160 when bidding opened, covered in our [NSE IPO](/blog/nse-ipo-drhp-2026-india-largest-ever) analysis.

**The monthly F&O expiry falls on 29 September, the last Tuesday of the month**, under the timings explained in our [closing auction session](/blog/closing-auction-session-sebi-fno-timings-2026) piece. September flash PMI, August infrastructure output, weekly foreign exchange reserves and bank credit growth all land in the same five-session week.

**The domestic policy setting has not changed: the repo rate is 5.25% and August CPI came in at 4.82% with food inflation at 5.95%**, which keeps a rate cut off the table for now, as our [will the RBI cut rates in 2026](/blog/will-rbi-cut-rates-2026) analysis sets out.

## Risks to monitor

<Callout type="risk" title="The tariff clock started on 18 September">

The Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act directs the President to raise duties within 30 days of enactment on imports from significant purchasers of Russian energy. India bought roughly 2.08 million barrels a day of Russian crude in August 2026, about 45% of its imports, which puts the country squarely inside the law's scope. The rate is capped at 100% and the list is reassessed every 180 days.

</Callout>

The second risk runs the other way. **A durable easing in the Gulf would take the remaining war premium out of oil**, and the same index that fell through six weeks of energy shock would recover fastest on its resolution.

The third is earnings. **The October results season is the first to carry a full quarter of expensive fuel and freight in the cost line**, which is where margin damage shows up rather than in a week of index moves. This is general information, not investment advice.

Six down weeks with foreign selling, a currency near its record low and oil above $100 for most of it is a punishing combination, and the index is down 11.5% rather than 25%. The buyer on the other side of nearly every one of those sale tickets was an Indian household's monthly SIP.
