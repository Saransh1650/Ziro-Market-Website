Earnings season went out on a high. **The Nifty is above 24,300 and the Sensex near 77,900 as of July 24, 2026, as Infosys shares jump on a full-year guidance raise that caps a strong week of results, even as the Strait of Hormuz standoff keeps Brent near $83 and the rupee near a record low.** The market has spent the week reconnecting with fundamentals, and it liked what it saw.

The Infosys raise was the exclamation point. After TCS, Wipro and HCLTech steadied the mood and Reliance and HDFC Bank added their weight, the sector's guidance bellwether lifting its outlook gave the week a confident close.

## What Is Happening

The market is finishing earnings season on the front foot. **With Infosys raising its guidance and the rest of the big names having reported steady numbers, the Nifty has pushed above 24,300, its firmest level in weeks.** The advance is broader than the IT-only gains of a week ago, with financials and energy joining in.

Infosys did the heavy lifting today. Its shares jumped after it lifted its FY27 revenue guidance to 2 to 4%, the strongest signal of the whole IT season, detailed in our [IT Q1 FY27 scorecard](/blog/it-sector-q1-fy27-scorecard-2026). A raise from the sector's guidance bellwether tends to pull the whole IT index and the broader market with it.

The macro backdrop still sets a ceiling. Brent near $83 and the rupee near 96.1 keep the standoff in view, tracked across our [crude oil price today](/blog/crude-oil-price-today) and [rupee vs dollar today](/blog/rupee-dollar-today) pages, which is why even a strong earnings week has not produced a runaway rally.

## Why This Matters for Investors

A market driven by earnings is on firmer ground than one driven by headlines. **When results, and especially a guidance raise, lead the tape, prices reconnect with company fundamentals, a more durable basis for gains than a pause in a geopolitical standoff.** That is why this week feels more solid than the ones before it.

The breadth is the encouraging part. IT, financials and energy all contributing means the rally rests on more than one leg, as our [Reliance Q1 FY27 results](/blog/reliance-q1-fy27-results-2026) and [HDFC Bank Q1 FY27 results](/blog/hdfc-bank-q1-fy27-results-2026) coverage details, giving the market a firmer footing heading into August.

For long-term investors, the shift from oil to earnings, capped by an Infosys raise, is a reminder that fundamentals reassert themselves. The Gulf still matters, but a sector raising its outlook is a fact the market can price, unlike a standoff it can only guess at.

## What To Watch

The first thing to watch is the RBI on early August. **With earnings mostly done, the rate decision is the next big event, and a hold at 5.25% is the consensus after June's cut**, as our [RBI August 2026 MPC preview](/blog/rbi-mpc-august-2026-preview) explains.

The second is whether the earnings optimism holds. A strong week can fade if follow-through buying does not appear, so the market's behaviour into month-end will show if the mood is durable.

The third is oil and the rupee. Brent near $83 and a record-low rupee remain the ceiling, so any move in the Gulf would quickly overshadow the earnings mood.

## Risks to Monitor

The clearest risk is the Hormuz standoff. A fresh escalation would spike oil and pull attention straight back from earnings to geopolitics.

A second risk is that the rally has run ahead of itself. With the market at its firmest in weeks, any disappointment could prompt profit-taking.

The third is foreign outflows. With the rupee near a record low, sustained foreign selling would weigh on both stocks and the currency. This is general information, not investment advice.

A Nifty above 24,300 to close a strong earnings week is a market that has rediscovered its footing on fundamentals. Infosys gave it a confident send-off, but with the RBI next and the Gulf still unresolved, whether the optimism carries into August is the question that now takes over.
