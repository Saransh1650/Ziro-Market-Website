The market has found a new thing to trade on, and for once it is not oil. **The Nifty is firming near 24,230 and the Sensex near 77,700 as of July 21, 2026, as a heavy earnings week led by Reliance, HDFC Bank and, on Thursday, Infosys pulls attention back to fundamentals, even as the Strait of Hormuz standoff keeps Brent near $83 and the rupee near a record low.** After weeks driven by the Gulf, results are back in charge.

The shift matters because it gives the market a domestic anchor. When the biggest companies report steady numbers, investors have a reason to look past the geopolitics, at least for a week.

## What Is Happening

Earnings have become the story. **With Reliance and HDFC Bank reporting steady results and the IT majors already out, the market has a domestic reason to firm up, lifting the Nifty toward 24,230.** The tone is constructive rather than euphoric, fitting a week when good news is expected but not guaranteed.

Breadth has improved from the narrow, IT-only gains of last week. Reliance lifts the energy and consumption names, and steady bank results support financials, the heaviest sector in the index, so the advance is broader than the one that carried the market on IT alone.

The macro backdrop still lurks. Brent near $83 and the rupee near 96.1 keep a ceiling on the market, tracked across our [crude oil price today](/blog/crude-oil-price-today) and [rupee vs dollar today](/blog/rupee-dollar-today) pages, which is why even a strong earnings week has not produced a breakout.

## Why This Matters for Investors

A market trading on earnings is healthier than one trading only on headlines. **When results drive the tape, prices reconnect with company fundamentals, which is a more durable basis for gains than a pause in a geopolitical standoff.** That is the main reason this week feels steadier than the ones before it.

The breadth is the encouraging part. Financials and consumption joining IT means the rally rests on more than one leg, and a broad-based earnings season would give the market a firmer footing heading into August, as our [Reliance Q1 FY27 results](/blog/reliance-q1-fy27-results-2026) and [HDFC Bank Q1 FY27 results](/blog/hdfc-bank-q1-fy27-results-2026) coverage details.

For long-term investors, the shift from oil to earnings is a reminder that fundamentals eventually reassert themselves. The Gulf still matters, but a company reporting real profit growth is a fact the market can price, unlike a standoff it can only guess at.

## What To Watch

The first thing to watch is Infosys on July 23. **As the only large IT firm to give explicit full-year guidance, its outlook is the single biggest earnings event of the week**, previewed in our [Infosys Q1 FY27 results](/blog/infosys-q1-fy27-results-preview-2026) piece and set in context by our [IT Q1 FY27 scorecard](/blog/it-sector-q1-fy27-scorecard-2026).

The second is the rest of bank earnings. ICICI, Axis and Kotak report this week, and with financials the heaviest index weight, their margins and asset quality will shape the Nifty.

The third is oil and the rupee. Brent near $83 and a record-low rupee remain the ceiling on the market, so any move in the Gulf would quickly overshadow the earnings mood.

## Risks to Monitor

The clearest risk is an earnings disappointment. With expectations having crept up, a weak Infosys guide or a soft bank result could quickly reverse the week's gains.

A second risk is the Hormuz standoff. A fresh escalation would spike oil and pull attention straight back from earnings to geopolitics.

The third is foreign outflows. With the rupee near a record low, sustained foreign selling would weigh on both stocks and the currency. This is general information, not investment advice.

A Nifty near 24,230 on a busy earnings week is a market getting back to what it is supposed to do: pricing companies, not just crises. Whether the firmer tone holds now rests on Infosys and the banks, with the Gulf still lurking as the one thing that could take the week's optimism away.
