The market found a small reason to breathe. **The Nifty is edging up near 24,120 and the Sensex near 77,300 as of July 16, 2026, led by IT after Wipro's steady Q1 guide, though the Strait of Hormuz standoff keeping oil near $85 and the rupee at a record low near 96 caps the gains.** It is a cautious lift, not a turn.

The tone has shifted from defensive to watchful. After steadying earlier in the week, the market is leaning on IT for support while it waits to see how the Gulf standoff and the rest of earnings season play out.

## What Is Happening

The market is being carried by a single sector. **After Wipro reported a soft but steady quarter with a flat guide, IT led the tape higher, lifting the Nifty toward 24,120 even as oil-sensitive sectors lagged.** The breadth is narrow, which is why the move reads as relief rather than a broad recovery.

Sector performance still tells a story of a squeeze. Auto, metals, and financials remain under pressure from costly crude and the prospect of the RBI staying tight after June inflation topped 4%. IT is the exception, drawing a double benefit from Wipro's reassurance and a weak rupee that flatters exporters earning in dollars.

The macro backdrop has not softened. The rupee near a record low, Brent above $85, and inflation at 4.38% still form a self-reinforcing squeeze, tracked across our [crude oil price today](/blog/crude-oil-price-today) and [rupee vs dollar today](/blog/rupee-dollar-today) pages, which is why an IT-led bounce cannot become a decisive rally.

## Why This Matters for Investors

Earnings are starting to matter again alongside the oil headlines. **Wipro's steady guide shows that when a result reassures, it can move the market even against a heavy macro backdrop**, a sign that the domestic story is not fully buried under the Gulf standoff.

The IT leadership is a useful signal. Its outperformance shows how a weak rupee redistributes winners and losers, cushioning exporters even as it hurts importers and consumers. That dynamic will keep mattering through earnings season, with Infosys on July 23 the next and biggest test, previewed in our [Infosys Q1 FY27 results](/blog/infosys-q1-fy27-results-preview-2026) piece.

For long-term investors, a firmer, narrow market is healthier than the crash that preceded it, but it still rests on an unresolved conflict. The direction from here depends heavily on whether the Hormuz standoff eases or escalates.

## What To Watch

The first thing to watch is the Strait of Hormuz and oil. **Because the market's caution rests on the standoff, any de-escalation could broaden the rally, while a confirmed closure would likely wipe out the IT-led gains.**

The second is Infosys earnings on July 23. As the largest of the big three reporters, its full-year guidance will confirm or complicate the stabilising picture Wipro just offered.

The third is the rupee and foreign flows. With the currency at a record low, any heavy foreign selling would pressure both stocks and the rupee further.

## Risks to Monitor

The clearest risk is an escalation over Hormuz. A confirmed, sustained closure would spike oil and hit Indian equities hard, IT tailwind or not.

A second risk is persistent inflation. With CPI above target and oil high, the RBI is likely to stay cautious, a headwind for rate-sensitive sectors.

The third is foreign outflows. Sustained selling by foreign investors as the rupee falls would weigh on the market. This is general information, not investment advice.

A Nifty edging up near 24,120 on IT relief is a market taking what support it can find. The Wipro read helped, and Infosys is next, but until the Strait of Hormuz question resolves, every up day is likely to stay narrow and provisional, one headline away from being undone.
