The market got the signal it was hoping for. **The Nifty is up near 24,420 and the Sensex near 78,150 as of July 31, 2026, in a relief rally after the US Federal Reserve held rates but opened the door to a September cut, a dovish tone that softened the dollar and helped the rupee firm off its record low toward 95.8.** IT, banks and other rate-sensitive stocks led the move.

After a week of waiting through expiry and the Fed, the market finally has a direction, and for now it is up. The question is whether it holds.

## What Is Happening

The Fed did the heavy lifting. **By holding rates while opening the door to a September cut, the Fed gave markets the dovish signal they wanted, weakening the dollar and lifting risk appetite worldwide**, as our [US Fed decision July 2026](/blog/us-fed-decision-july-2026-india-impact) piece laid out. Indian equities joined the global relief.

The rupee was the clearest winner. It firmed toward 95.8 from around 96.1 as the dollar softened, easing the pressure that has weighed on it for weeks, as our [rupee vs dollar today](/blog/rupee-dollar-today) page tracks. A calmer rupee also takes some strain off importers and inflation.

The leadership tells the story. Rate-sensitive sectors, banks, autos and real estate, rallied on the prospect of lower global rates, while IT gained on the risk-on mood, making this a broad-based up day rather than the narrow, single-sector moves of earlier weeks.

## Why This Matters for Investors

A dovish Fed changes the backdrop. **Lower US rates ahead mean a softer dollar, which eases pressure on the rupee and can slow or reverse the foreign selling that has dogged Indian markets**, a dynamic covered in our [how to read FII and DII activity](/blog/how-to-read-fii-dii-activity) guide. If foreign investors turn buyers, that is a meaningful tailwind.

It also helps the RBI. A dovish Fed gives the RBI more room to cut later in the year without pressuring the rupee, which matters ahead of its decision next week, as our [RBI August 2026 MPC preview](/blog/rbi-mpc-august-2026-preview) explains. The two central banks are linked through the currency.

For long-term investors, the relief is welcome but rests on follow-through. A single dovish meeting lifts sentiment; a durable rally needs foreign buying to return, oil to stay contained, and the domestic story to keep delivering.

## What To Watch

The first thing to watch is foreign flows. **Whether the dovish Fed turns foreign investors from sellers into buyers is the key signal for how far this rally can run.**

The second is the RBI next week, where a hold at 5.25% is expected but the tone now matters more given the Fed's opening.

The third is oil and the rupee. Brent near $83 from the Strait of Hormuz standoff remains the ceiling, so any spike would quickly test the relief.

## Risks to Monitor

The clearest risk is that the rally fades without follow-through. Relief rallies can reverse if foreign buying does not materialise or the mood shifts.

A second risk is the Hormuz standoff. A fresh escalation would spike oil, pressure the rupee again, and overshadow the Fed relief.

The third is disappointment at the RBI or in remaining results, which could stall the momentum. This is general information, not investment advice.

A Nifty past 24,400 on a dovish Fed is the market exhaling after a tense week. The dollar has softened, the rupee has bounced, and the mood has lifted, but the rally now has to prove it can last, with foreign flows, the RBI and the ever-present Gulf standoff the tests it must pass.
