A day after the crash, the market found its feet. **On Thursday, July 9, 2026, the Nifty climbed back above 24,000 to around 24,013 and the Sensex rose to about 76,968, each recovering roughly 0.6%, as strong Asian cues and bargain-hunting clawed back part of the previous session's 2% plunge, though oil near $79 a barrel kept the bounce in check.** After the close, a solid set of TCS results added to the steadier tone.

This was a relief rally, not an all-clear. The Nifty recovered only a fraction of what it lost, and the same force that caused the crash, a high oil price on the US-Iran conflict, is still very much in play.

## What Happened

The tone flipped from fear to cautious relief. **Tracking firm Asian markets led by Japan's Nikkei, Indian equities opened higher and held their gains, with Eternal, Bharti Airtel, Trent, Titan, and M&M among the top performers**, as buyers returned after the sharp sell-off. The Nifty reclaiming the 24,000 mark was the session's key psychological win.

The recovery had a ceiling, though. Crude oil stayed elevated near $79 a barrel for Brent as the US-Iran conflict rumbled on, keeping inflation and rupee worries alive and limiting how far the bounce could run, as tracked on our [crude oil price today](/blog/crude-oil-price-today) page. Investors welcomed the rebound but stayed guarded.

The day's other big event came after the bell. TCS opened the Q1 FY27 earnings season with a solid report, posting net profit of Rs 13,349 crore and a strong $9.5 billion order book, detailed in our [TCS Q1 FY27 results](/blog/tcs-q1-fy27-results-preview-2026) coverage. As the first major IT firm to report, its steadiness added to the calmer mood.

## Why This Matters for Investors

The bounce shows the crash was a shock, not a collapse. **A 2% plunge followed by a partial recovery suggests investors read the sell-off as a geopolitics-driven event rather than a change in the economy's fundamentals**, which is why bargain-hunters returned so quickly. But the modest size of the rebound signals lingering caution.

The oil overhang is the crucial caveat. Because crude stayed high, the market could not fully shake off the fear that hit it the day before, a reminder that the recovery is hostage to the Strait of Hormuz and the direction of oil, not just to domestic buying.

For long-term investors, a steadying market plus a dependable TCS result is an encouraging combination after a jarring session. The Q1 FY27 earnings season, now underway, offers a fresh set of fundamentals for the market to trade on, which could help it look past the geopolitical noise if the numbers hold up.

## Market Reaction

Breadth improved as beaten-down names bounced, and the leadership from consumption and telecom stocks pointed to a broad-based relief rather than a narrow push. **A recovery led by a mix of sectors, rather than a single one, suggests the buying was genuine bargain-hunting after the crash.**

Reclaiming 24,000 is the technical takeaway. After slicing below its 200-day average the day before, the Nifty pushing back above the round number will have traders watching whether it can also retake the moving average and hold it in the coming sessions.

## What Investors Should Watch

The first thing to watch is oil and the Strait of Hormuz. **Because the crash and the capped rebound both trace back to crude, the direction of oil prices will decide whether this recovery extends or fades.**

The second is the earnings season. With TCS setting a steady tone and Infosys due on July 23, IT results will shape sentiment, and strong numbers could help the market look past the geopolitical stress.

The third is the rupee and foreign flows. The rupee near 95.6 is under pressure from high oil, tracked on our [rupee vs dollar today](/blog/rupee-dollar-today) page, and any heavy foreign selling would weigh on the recovery.

## Risks to Monitor

The clearest risk is a fresh escalation in the Gulf. Any further disruption near the Strait of Hormuz would lift oil again and could quickly reverse the rebound.

A second risk is sticky, higher oil. If crude holds near or above $79, the drag on India's import bill and inflation could cap equities for a while, keeping rallies shallow.

The third is a weak earnings follow-through. TCS steadied nerves, but a disappointing result from another major could revive the caution. This is general information, not investment advice.

A partial bounce that reclaims 24,000 is a reassuring answer to the previous day's crash, but it is not a resolution. Until oil cools and the Gulf calms, the Indian market is likely to keep taking its cues from a narrow stretch of water far from Mumbai, with earnings season offering the main domestic counterweight.
