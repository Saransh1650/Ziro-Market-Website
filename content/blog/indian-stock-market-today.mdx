The market ended a turbulent week on a strong note. **In Friday's session on July 10, 2026, the Nifty rose 1.02% to 24,207 and the Sensex 1.08% to 77,569, a second straight day of gains as investors looked past the US-Iran conflict, cheaper oil helped, and IT and banking led a broad-based rally.** Both foreign and domestic institutions were buyers, a reassuring shift after a jittery start to the month.

The bounce did not quite erase the damage. Thanks to Wednesday's sharp crash, the Nifty still ended the week about 0.3% lower, a reminder that the recovery is real but incomplete.

## What Happened

The recovery broadened as the week ended. **IT and banking led Friday's advance, with the Nifty IT index up about 1.96% to 28,010, helped by TCS's steady Q1 results, and the Nifty Bank up about 1.39% to 58,046**, while PSU banks and realty joined a rally that touched most sectors. After a crash and a cautious bounce, Friday's move had the breadth of genuine risk appetite returning.

The backdrop turned friendlier. Crude eased from its mid-week peak, taking some pressure off the inflation and rupee worries that had driven the sell-off, as tracked on our [crude oil price today](/blog/crude-oil-price-today) page. Investors chose to look past the still-unresolved US-Iran conflict, betting that the worst of the shock was priced in.

The flows told the story. **Both foreign and domestic institutions were net buyers on Friday, with FIIs adding about Rs 2,604 crore and DIIs about Rs 2,020 crore**, a vote of confidence that helped the rupee steady near 95.33, covered on our [rupee vs dollar today](/blog/rupee-dollar-today) page.

## Why This Matters for Investors

The return of foreign buying is the week's most encouraging signal. **After a risk-off week, FIIs turning net buyers suggests overseas investors see the crash as a shock rather than a reason to exit India**, which supports both the market and the currency. Domestic institutions buying alongside them adds a second layer of support.

The IT rebound matters too. TCS opening the earnings season with solid numbers, detailed in our [TCS Q1 FY27 results](/blog/tcs-q1-fy27-results-preview-2026) coverage, steadied a sector that had been the market's weakest, and helped lift the whole index on Friday. It sets a more constructive tone into the rest of earnings season.

For long-term investors, a strong finish to a volatile week is welcome, but the real tests are just ahead. Inflation data, more IT results, and the unresolved Gulf conflict will decide whether this rebound builds into a fresh leg up or fades.

## Market Reaction

Breadth was the standout, with gains spread across IT, banking, PSU banks, and realty rather than concentrated in a few names. **A broad-based rally with institutional buying on both sides is a healthier setup than a narrow bounce**, suggesting the recovery has real conviction behind it.

Reclaiming the ground lost after the crash is the technical takeaway. The Nifty back above 24,200 will have traders watching whether it can retake its 200-day average and hold it, the level it lost during Wednesday's plunge.

## What Investors Should Watch

The first thing to watch is India's inflation data. **June CPI is due Monday and is expected to rise above the RBI's 4% target for the first time in 16 months, so a hotter-than-expected print could revive caution**, with WPI following on Tuesday.

The second is the earnings season. Wipro reports on July 16 and Infosys on July 23, and after TCS's steady start, their numbers will show whether the IT recovery is broad or narrow.

The third is oil and the rupee. The rebound leaned on crude easing, so any fresh flare-up in the Gulf that lifts oil would pressure both stocks and the currency again.

## Risks to Monitor

The clearest risk is a renewed oil spike. The US-Iran conflict is unresolved and shipping through the Strait of Hormuz remains disrupted, so crude could jump again on a single headline.

A second risk is a hot inflation print. If June CPI comes in well above 4%, it could dampen hopes for RBI rate support and weigh on rate-sensitive sectors.

The third is a weak earnings follow-through. TCS steadied nerves, but a disappointing result from Wipro or Infosys could revive the caution. This is general information, not investment advice.

A strong Friday and the return of foreign buying give the market momentum into a data-heavy week, but the same forces that caused the crash, oil and the Gulf conflict, have not gone away. Whether the Nifty can turn a two-day bounce into a durable recovery now rests on inflation, earnings, and a narrow stretch of water in the Middle East.
