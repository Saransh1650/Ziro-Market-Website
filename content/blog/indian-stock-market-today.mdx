Oil and American interest rates hit the market together. **The Sensex fell 1,247.71 points, or 1.68%, to 73,580.54 on 24 September 2026, and the Nifty 50 lost 383.70 points, or 1.64%, to 23,063.10, its lowest close in about five months.** Every major sector index ended lower, and investors lost about Rs 4.06 lakh crore of market value in a session.

The damage was concentrated in finance. **The Nifty Financial Services index fell 2.39%, its steepest drop in about three months**, and Motilal Oswal Financial Services slid nearly 10% at one point, with insurance stocks also under pressure from fresh distribution proposals.

Friday brought a partial rebound: **the Sensex regained 315.20 points to 73,895.74 and the Nifty 50 rose 77.40 points to 23,140.50 on 25 September**, as hopes of a phased US-Iran deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz lifted risk appetite and the rupee firmed 19 paise to 95.80.

The trigger for Thursday's fall came from outside India. **Brent crude rose about 2.25% to $105.40 a barrel, and the US 10-year Treasury yield climbed to 5.106%, its highest since 2007**, as talks between US and Iranian negotiators left the oil supply outlook unsettled.

## What moved the market?

Oil moved first, in the wrong direction. **Brent rose 2.25% to $105.40 on 24 September, reversing the 11-day low it touched on Monday**, tracked daily on our [crude oil price today](/blog/crude-oil-price-today) page. India imports more than 90% of the crude it burns, so a bounce like this feeds straight into the import bill, inflation and the rupee.

The second push was American. **The US 10-year yield rose about 14 basis points to 5.106%, and the 2-year briefly crossed 4.9%**, the highest since 2007 and May 2024 respectively, which is why the move matters for a market that depends on foreign flows. Our [US 10-year yield at 5%](/blog/us-10-year-yield-5-percent-india-impact) explainer covers the mechanism.

The rupee followed both. **The rupee fell 0.2% to settle near 95.96 to the dollar**, as our [rupee versus dollar today](/blog/rupee-dollar-today) page tracks.

## Who is buying and who is selling?

The year-long pattern held, at a larger scale. **Foreign institutional investors sold a net Rs 5,027 crore of Indian equities on 24 September 2026 while domestic institutions bought about Rs 4,301 crore**, according to provisional exchange data.

The bigger picture is the same one that has defined 2026. **Foreign portfolio investors have pulled about Rs 2.45 lakh crore out of Indian equities this year, while SIP inflows hit a record Rs 32,297 crore in August**, the gap that has kept the index far above where it would otherwise be. The mechanics are in [how to read FII and DII activity](/blog/how-to-read-fii-dii-activity).

## NSE's debut: a quiet listing on a loud day

The National Stock Exchange began trading on the BSE the same day. **It listed at Rs 1,800, a 0.84% premium to the Rs 1,785 issue price, touched Rs 1,869 and closed at Rs 1,817, about 1.8% above the issue price**, for a market capitalisation of roughly Rs 4.59 lakh crore.

## What comes next?

**The monthly F&O expiry falls on 29 September, the last Tuesday of the month**, under the timings explained in our [closing auction session](/blog/closing-auction-session-sebi-fno-timings-2026) piece, and the Reserve Bank of India's policy decision follows on 7 October.

**The domestic policy setting has not changed: the repo rate is 5.25% and August CPI came in at 4.82% with food inflation at 5.95%**, which keeps a rate cut off the table, as our [will the RBI cut rates in 2026](/blog/will-rbi-cut-rates-2026) analysis sets out.

## Risks to monitor

<Callout type="risk" title="The tariff clock is running">

The Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act, signed on 18 September, directs the President to raise duties within 30 days on imports from significant purchasers of Russian energy. India bought roughly 2.08 million barrels a day of Russian crude in August 2026, about 45% of its imports, which puts it inside the law's scope. The rate is capped at 100%.

</Callout>

The second risk is that oil now depends on talks that have not produced a deal. **If the US-Iran negotiations stall, Brent can stay above $100 and keep US yields elevated, which is the combination that hit the market on Thursday.**

The third is earnings. **The October results season is the first to carry a full quarter of expensive fuel and freight in the cost line**, which is where margin damage shows up rather than in a day of index moves. This is general information, not investment advice.

A 1.6% fall is large by recent standards, but the buyer on the other side of most foreign sale tickets this year has been an Indian household's monthly SIP, and that has not changed.
