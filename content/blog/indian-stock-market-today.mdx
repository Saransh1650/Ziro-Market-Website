The market closed the week on a quiet but positive note, with the heavy lifting done by banks and carmakers. **The Sensex ended the week near 77,100 and the Nifty 50 at about 24,056 in the last session on Thursday, June 25, 2026, capping a weekly gain of roughly 1.65% for the Nifty on cheaper oil, renewed foreign buying, and India-US trade hopes.** Friday was a Muharram holiday, so Thursday was the final trading day.

## What Happened

The week belonged to rate-sensitive and consumption stocks. **Banking and automobile names drove the rally, with Mahindra & Mahindra rising about 3.85% and Maruti Suzuki about 3.81%, while Nestle India also gained.** On the other side, Power Grid fell about 2.20%, Tech Mahindra about 1.49%, and Bharti Airtel about 1.46%, leaving IT and metals as the laggards.

The backdrop did the work. Crude oil tumbled to its lowest since February as the Iran ceasefire reopened the Strait of Hormuz, a direct positive for an oil importer like India, which you can track on our [crude oil price today](/blog/crude-oil-price-today) page. **Foreign institutional investors, sellers for much of 2026, turned buyers again**, and optimism built around an India-US trade deal ahead of the July 24 tariff deadline.

The final session on Thursday was modest, with the Sensex up about 109 points to near 77,100 and the Nifty adding 34 points to about 24,056. With Friday shut for Muharram, the steady weekly gain of around 1.65% for the Nifty was locked in a day early.

## Why This Matters for Investors

The week showed a healthier kind of rally than the index level alone suggests. **A rotation into banks and autos, supported by foreign buying, points to broad-based confidence rather than a narrow move led by a single heavyweight.** Rate-sensitive sectors leading is often read as a bet that the rate and inflation backdrop is improving, helped by the oil crash.

It also underlined how much the external picture is driving Indian equities. Cheaper oil, a steadier rupee, and trade-deal hopes are all global or policy stories, and right now they matter more to the market than any single set of domestic numbers.

For longer-term investors, the return of FII buying is the most encouraging signal. After persistent foreign outflows weighed on the market through 2026, a genuine shift back to buying would be a meaningful change in the demand picture, though one good week does not confirm a trend.

## Market Reaction

Breadth was constructive, with banks, autos, and select consumption names carrying the indices while IT and metals dragged. **The clear leadership of domestic, rate-sensitive sectors over export-facing IT suggests investors are leaning into the India growth story while staying cautious on global tech demand.**

The muted final session reflects a market consolidating its weekly gains rather than chasing them higher. With a long weekend created by the Muharram holiday, some traders also trimmed risk ahead of the break, a normal pattern before a market closure.

## What Investors Should Watch

The first thing to watch is the India-US trade deal. **With the July 24 tariff deadline approaching, any progress or setback in the talks will likely be the single biggest driver when markets reopen.** Headlines from Delhi or Washington can move the Nifty quickly.

The second is oil and the rupee. The recent rally leaned heavily on cheaper crude, so any rebound in oil prices would test the market's resilience and pressure the currency, which you can follow on our [rupee vs dollar today](/blog/rupee-dollar-today) page.

The third is the calendar. The AMFI large-cap and mid-cap reshuffle list is due in early July, and the Q1 FY27 earnings season starts soon after, both of which will shape sector and stock moves into July.

## Risks to Monitor

The clearest risk is that the trade talks stall. If a deal slips past July 24 and the temporary US tariff snaps back, the optimism behind the recent gains could unwind.

A second risk is an oil rebound. The market's strength has been tied to falling crude, so a flare-up around the Strait of Hormuz would remove a key support.

The third is global. A hawkish US Federal Reserve, a strong dollar, and rising yields remain headwinds, and any fresh shock from those could overwhelm the domestic good news. This is general information, not investment advice.

A 1.65% weekly gain for the Nifty, led by banks and autos and backed by returning foreign money, is a solid close to a volatile month. Whether it carries into July depends, as so much has this year, on oil, the rupee, and the fate of the trade deal.
