The week ended on a steadier note than it began. **The Nifty closed near 24,190 and the Sensex near 77,520 in the week to July 17, 2026, up about 0.6%, as a run of reassuring IT results from TCS, Wipro and HCLTech offset the Strait of Hormuz standoff that kept oil near $84 and the rupee near a record low.** It was a narrow, IT-led gain rather than a broad advance.

With the market shut for the weekend, attention has already moved to the week ahead, where Infosys and its all-important guidance are the main event.

## What Happened This Week

The story of the week was IT earnings. **TCS, Wipro and HCLTech all reported Q1 FY27 results without disappointing, and three steady reads in a row lifted a sector that fell about 30% in the first half of 2026.** Wipro held its margin and gave a flat guide, and HCLTech went further by keeping its full-year guidance unchanged, the firmest signal of the season.

The breadth stayed narrow, though. Auto, metals and financials lagged all week, weighed down by costly crude and the prospect of the RBI staying cautious after June inflation topped 4%. The gains were concentrated in IT, which drew a double benefit from the earnings relief and a weak rupee that flatters exporters earning in dollars.

The macro backdrop eased only at the margin. Brent slipped toward $84 and the rupee steadied near 96.2 over the week, tracked across our [crude oil price today](/blog/crude-oil-price-today) and [rupee vs dollar today](/blog/rupee-dollar-today) pages, but with the Hormuz standoff unresolved, neither moved enough to change the picture.

## Why This Matters for Investors

The week showed earnings can still move the market. **When results reassure, they can lift sentiment even against a heavy macro backdrop, which is what let IT carry the index higher despite oil and the rupee working against it.** That is a healthier dynamic than the geopolitics-only market of a week earlier.

The IT relief has a limit, though. A rally led by one sector is fragile, and the broader market cannot sustain gains while oil-sensitive sectors stay weak. For a durable move higher, either the Hormuz standoff needs to ease or the earnings strength needs to broaden beyond IT.

For long-term investors, a steady week is welcome after the crash that opened July, but the market is still hostage to the Gulf. The direction from here depends heavily on whether the standoff de-escalates or flares up again.

## The Week Ahead

The main event is Infosys on July 23. **As the only large Indian IT firm to give explicit full-year revenue guidance, its outlook will confirm or complicate the stabilising picture the other three have painted**, previewed in our [Infosys Q1 FY27 results](/blog/infosys-q1-fy27-results-preview-2026) piece.

Beyond earnings, the Strait of Hormuz and oil remain the swing factor for the whole market, and any move in Brent will ripple through the rupee and inflation. Foreign investor flows, which have pressured the rupee, are the other thing to watch.

Further out, the RBI's early-August rate decision is coming into view, with a hold at 5.25% the consensus after June's cut, as our [RBI August 2026 MPC preview](/blog/rbi-mpc-august-2026-preview) explains.

## Risks to Monitor

The clearest risk is an escalation over Hormuz. A confirmed, sustained closure would spike oil and hit Indian equities hard, IT tailwind or not.

A second risk is a disappointing Infosys guide. If the sector leader cuts its outlook, the IT-led relief of this week could unwind quickly.

The third is foreign outflows. Sustained selling by foreign investors as the rupee stays weak would weigh on both stocks and the currency. This is general information, not investment advice.

A Nifty near 24,190 after a steady week is a market that has found its footing without finding conviction. IT did its job, but Infosys, the Gulf and the rupee will decide whether next week extends the gain or takes it back.
