Oil gave the market a Monday it needed. **The Sensex rose 564.03 points, or 0.76%, to 74,858.99 on 21 September 2026, and the Nifty 50 gained 67.90 points, or 0.29%, to 23,414.30, as Brent crude fell about 2% to an 11-day low near $101 a barrel.** Pharma, FMCG and realty led the sectors, while telecom and some Adani names dragged.

The headline hid a softer market underneath. **The Nifty Midcap index fell 0.29% and the Smallcap index 0.07%**, the reverse of Friday, when smaller stocks outran the benchmarks, so Monday's gain was concentrated in a handful of heavyweights.

The trigger was diplomatic as much as physical. Saudi Arabian oil exports recovered after the 11 September shutdown of its East-West pipeline, and President Trump said he was open to meeting Iran's President Masoud Pezeshkian at the UN General Assembly this week.

## What moved the market?

Oil moved first, in the helpful direction. **Brent fell about 2% to an 11-day low near $101 on 21 September 2026, after settling at $103.87 on Friday and peaking at $108.75 in mid-September**, tracked daily on our [crude oil price today](/blog/crude-oil-price-today) page. India imports more than 90% of the crude it burns, so every $10 off the barrel is worth billions of dollars a year to the current account.

## Who is buying and who is selling?

The year-long pattern held. **Foreign institutional investors sold a net Rs 576.20 crore of Indian equities on 21 September 2026 while domestic institutions bought Rs 2,797.30 crore**, according to provisional exchange data.

The bigger picture is the same one that has defined 2026. **Foreign portfolio investors have pulled about Rs 2.45 lakh crore out of Indian equities this year, while SIP inflows hit a record Rs 32,297 crore in August**, the gap that has kept the index about 11% below its January record rather than far more. The mechanics are in [how to read FII and DII activity](/blog/how-to-read-fii-dii-activity).

## What comes next?

**The National Stock Exchange lists on the BSE on 24 September 2026 after its Rs 22,562 crore IPO closed 5.7 times subscribed**, with institutions bidding 12.7 times their quota and the grey market premium down to about Rs 48, covered in our [NSE IPO](/blog/nse-ipo-drhp-2026-india-largest-ever) analysis.

**The monthly F&O expiry falls on 29 September, the last Tuesday of the month**, under the timings explained in our [closing auction session](/blog/closing-auction-session-sebi-fno-timings-2026) piece, and the Reserve Bank of India's policy decision follows on 7 October.

**The domestic policy setting has not changed: the repo rate is 5.25% and August CPI came in at 4.82% with food inflation at 5.95%**, which keeps a rate cut off the table for now, as our [will the RBI cut rates in 2026](/blog/will-rbi-cut-rates-2026) analysis sets out.

## Risks to monitor

<Callout type="risk" title="The tariff clock is running">

The Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act, signed on 18 September, directs the President to raise duties within 30 days on imports from significant purchasers of Russian energy. India bought roughly 2.08 million barrels a day of Russian crude in August 2026, about 45% of its imports, which puts it squarely inside the law's scope. The rate is capped at 100%.

</Callout>

The second risk is that Monday's relief depends on a meeting that has not happened. **If the US-Iran talks around the UN come to nothing, the several dollars that diplomacy took out of the oil price can return just as quickly.**

The third is earnings. **The October results season is the first to carry a full quarter of expensive fuel and freight in the cost line**, which is where margin damage shows up rather than in a day of index moves. This is general information, not investment advice.

A 564-point Sensex gain does not undo six weeks of losses, but it shows how quickly the market responds when its biggest external input, oil, moves the right way. The buyer on the other side of most foreign sale tickets this year has been an Indian household's monthly SIP, and that has not changed.
