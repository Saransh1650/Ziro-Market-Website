Earnings season delivered, and the market rewarded it. **The Nifty closed near 24,300 and the Sensex near 77,900 in the week to July 24, 2026, up about 1%, its best week in some time, as a strong Q1 FY27 season from IT, Reliance and the big banks lifted sentiment, even as the Strait of Hormuz standoff kept Brent near $83 and the rupee near a record low.** After weeks driven by the Gulf, results carried the market.

With the market shut for the weekend, attention has already turned to the week ahead, where the RBI's rate decision takes over from earnings as the main event.

## What Happened This Week

Results were the whole story. **The Q1 FY27 season peaked, and it went better than feared: all four big IT firms reported without disappointing, Infosys raised its full-year guidance, and Reliance and the top banks delivered steady-to-strong numbers.** That combination gave the market a domestic reason to rally after weeks of trading on oil headlines.

The breadth was the encouraging part. IT led early, then financials joined as HDFC Bank and ICICI Bank reported healthy results, and Reliance lifted the energy and consumption names, as our [IT Q1 FY27 scorecard](/blog/it-sector-q1-fy27-scorecard-2026) and [Reliance Q1 FY27 results](/blog/reliance-q1-fy27-results-2026) coverage details. A rally resting on several sectors is sturdier than one leaning on IT alone.

The macro backdrop still set a ceiling. Brent near $83 and the rupee near 96.1 kept the Gulf standoff in view, tracked across our [crude oil price today](/blog/crude-oil-price-today) and [rupee vs dollar today](/blog/rupee-dollar-today) pages, which is why even a strong week produced a gain of about 1% rather than a breakout.

## Why This Matters for Investors

A market trading on earnings is on firmer ground. **When results drive the tape, prices reconnect with company fundamentals, a more durable basis for gains than a pause in a geopolitical standoff.** That is why this week felt more solid than the volatile, oil-driven weeks that opened July.

The bank results carried extra weight. Financials are the heaviest sector in the index, so steady numbers from HDFC Bank and ICICI Bank, compared in our [ICICI Bank Q1 FY27 results](/blog/icici-bank-q1-fy27-results-2026) coverage, matter for the whole market, not just the sector. A healthy credit cycle underpins the rally.

For long-term investors, the shift from oil to earnings is a reminder that fundamentals eventually reassert themselves. The Gulf still matters, but a season of real profit growth is a fact the market can price, unlike a standoff it can only guess at.

## The Week Ahead

The main event is the RBI in early August. **With earnings mostly done, the rate decision takes over as the market's focus, and a hold at 5.25% is the consensus after June's cut**, as our [RBI August 2026 MPC preview](/blog/rbi-mpc-august-2026-preview) explains.

More results still land, including State Bank of India and the auto majors, which will round out the Q1 FY27 picture. The monthly F&O expiry at the end of the month can also add volatility.

Oil and the rupee remain the swing factors. Brent near $83 and a record-low rupee are the ceiling on the market, so any move in the Gulf would quickly overshadow the earnings mood.

## Risks to Monitor

The clearest risk is the Hormuz standoff. A fresh escalation would spike oil and pull attention straight back from earnings to geopolitics.

A second risk is that the rally has run ahead of itself. After the best week in some time, any disappointment at the RBI or in remaining results could prompt profit-taking.

The third is foreign outflows. With the rupee near a record low, sustained foreign selling would weigh on both stocks and the currency. This is general information, not investment advice.

A Nifty near 24,300 to close its best earnings week is a market that has rediscovered its footing on fundamentals. The season delivered, but with the RBI next and the Gulf still unresolved, whether the optimism carries into August is the question that now takes over.
