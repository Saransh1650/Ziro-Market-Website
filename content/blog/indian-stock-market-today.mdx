The market closed the week on the front foot, and technology led the way back. **In the last session on Friday, July 3, 2026, the Nifty 50 rose about 0.39% to hold above 24,000, near 24,100, and the Sensex added about 0.34% to around 77,180, capping a week of gains driven by a sharp rebound in IT, healthcare and pharma.** A calmer world and cheaper oil kept buyers in control.

## What Happened

The week belonged to the sectors that had been left behind. **IT, healthcare and pharma led the gains, with HCL Technologies jumping over 6% intraday and Tech Mahindra also rising sharply**, a striking reversal for an IT pack that had dragged the market only days earlier. State Bank of India and Kotak Mahindra Bank were among the modest laggards.

The backdrop stayed supportive. A US-Iran ceasefire held, keeping crude oil cheap, which is a direct positive for oil-importing India, as tracked on our [crude oil price today](/blog/crude-oil-price-today) page. Global cues helped too, with Wall Street and Asian markets firm, keeping investors in a risk-on mood.

By Friday's close, the Nifty was comfortably back above 24,000 near 24,100, and the Sensex near 77,180, both extending the week's advance. With the weekend ahead, the market locked in a solid week before the big July events.

## Why This Matters for Investors

The IT rebound is the week's most telling move. **After the sector was hammered on weak global tech cues, its sharp recovery suggests investors are willing to buy the dip ahead of the Q1 FY27 earnings season**, betting that the worst of the demand worries may be priced in. That season, starting mid-July, will test the optimism.

The broader message is that the market is trading on a friendlier backdrop. Cheap oil, a holding ceasefire, and firm global markets have combined to keep Indian equities above 24,000, a level they struggled to hold through late June.

For long-term investors, a strong week led by a beaten-down sector is encouraging, but the real tests, earnings and the trade deadline, are still ahead. The tone into July is positive, but it rests on external calm that can change quickly.

## Market Reaction

Breadth was constructive, with IT, healthcare and pharma leading and only a few heavyweights lagging. **The leadership of previously weak IT signals a relief rally and bargain-hunting rather than fresh euphoria**, as money rotated back into the sector that had been sold hardest.

Holding above 24,000 into the weekend is the key technical takeaway. After whipsawing around the level in late June, the Nifty has now spent the week above it, which traders will watch for follow-through when trading resumes.

## What Investors Should Watch

The first thing to watch is the Q1 FY27 earnings season. **It begins mid-July with IT majors like TCS, so their results and guidance will show whether this week's IT rebound is justified or premature.**

The second is the July 24 India-US trade deadline. With the tariff clock ticking, any progress or setback in the talks will move the market, as covered in our [stock market in July 2026](/blog/stock-market-july-2026-what-to-watch) guide.

The third is oil and the rupee. The rally has leaned on cheap crude, so any rebound in oil would pressure both stocks and the currency, which slipped to a fresh low this week.

## Risks to Monitor

The clearest risk is a breakdown in the US-Iran ceasefire, which would likely lift oil and hit Indian equities given how much the recent gains have relied on cheap crude.

A second risk is a weak IT earnings season. After this week's rebound, disappointing guidance from the majors could quickly reverse the sector's gains.

The third is the trade deal. A miss on the July 24 deadline would let the temporary US tariff snap back, denting the optimism supporting exporters. This is general information, not investment advice.

A week of gains led by a rebounding IT sector is a confident start to July, but the month's defining events, earnings and the trade deal, will decide whether the Nifty can build on its hold above 24,000 or slip back into the range that defined late June.
