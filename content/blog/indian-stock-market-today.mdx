The market found its footing the moment trade talks did. **The Nifty 50 rose about 197 points or 0.83% to 24,021.65 on June 24, 2026, and the Sensex jumped 790.54 points or 1.04% to 76,991.22, as hopes around an India-US trade deal sparked a sharp rebound led by IT, realty, and bank stocks.** The gains clawed back most of the previous day's heavy fall in a single session.

![Indian markets on 24 June 2026: Nifty closed at 24,021.65 up 0.83%, Sensex at 76,991.22 up 1.04%, led by IT and realty](/images/indian-market-today.svg)

The bounce came a day after a brutal slide, when the Sensex had dropped 893 points and the Nifty had sunk to 23,824. News that India and the US had wrapped up two days of trade talks flipped the mood back to risk-on.

## What Happened

The session was a clean reversal of the previous day's gloom. **The Nifty IT and Nifty Realty indices each climbed more than 2%, leading a broad recovery that lifted banking and financial stocks too.** IT, which had been the market's biggest drag earlier in the week after Accenture's weak guidance, turned into its biggest winner as buyers stepped in at lower levels.

The spark was trade. India and the US concluded two days of ministerial-level talks on the first phase of a bilateral trade agreement, with Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal meeting US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer. **The talks raced against a July 24 deadline, when Washington's temporary 10% tariff on trading partners is set to expire.** A framework agreed in February would cut US tariffs on Indian goods to 18% from 50%, so visible progress was read as a clear positive for exporters.

Among individual names, InterGlobe Aviation, Adani Enterprises, and Trent were among the top Nifty 50 gainers. The broader market lagged, though, with the Nifty MidCap up only about 0.10% and the SmallCap about 0.39%, a sign the buying was concentrated in heavyweight index stocks rather than spread evenly.

## Why This Matters for Investors

The day showed how much policy headlines now move this market. **A single update on trade talks was enough to reverse a 893-point Sensex fall, underlining how sensitive Indian equities are to the US tariff question.** With the July 24 deadline looming, every signal from the negotiating table is likely to swing sentiment in the weeks ahead.

It also revealed where the bargain-hunters are looking. The fact that IT and realty, two of the week's worst performers, led the rebound suggests investors are treating recent falls in those sectors as overdone rather than as the start of a deeper decline.

For long-term investors, a sharp one-day bounce is as unreliable a guide as a sharp one-day fall. The more useful takeaway is that the market is trading the trade deal both ways, rallying on hope and selling on doubt, which points to choppy sessions until there is a concrete outcome.

## Market Reaction

Breadth improved sharply from the day before, with IT, realty, banks, and financials all in the green. **The leadership of the previously battered IT and realty packs signalled a relief rally rather than fresh enthusiasm**, as money flowed back into the names that had been sold hardest.

The lagging mid- and small-caps tell the other half of the story. When large-caps run far ahead of the broader market, it usually means the move is being driven by index heavyweights tied to a specific catalyst, in this case the trade deal, rather than by widespread buying.

## What Investors Should Watch

The first thing to watch is the India-US trade deal itself. **With the July 24 tariff deadline now the key event, any sign the deal will or won't be finalised in time will be the single biggest driver for the market.** Headlines from both capitals can move the Nifty in either direction.

The second is whether the Nifty holds above 24,000. The index has now whipsawed around this level all week, so a sustained close above it would be more convincing than a single strong day.

The third is the Q1 FY27 earnings season starting in July. After Accenture's warning rattled IT, the guidance from TCS, Infosys, and HCLTech will show whether the sector's rebound is justified or premature.

## Risks to Monitor

The clearest risk is that the trade talks stall. If the two sides fail to reach a deal before July 24 and the 10% tariff snaps back, the optimism behind this rally could unwind just as quickly as it built.

A second risk is global. Bond yields, the US Federal Reserve's rate path, and oil prices all remain swing factors, and a fresh shock from any of them could overwhelm the domestic trade story.

The third is the narrow breadth. A rally led by a handful of heavyweights while mid- and small-caps lag can stall if the index leaders run out of steam, so the health of the broader market is worth watching.

One strong session does not confirm a trend any more than one weak one ends it, but June 24 is a clear reminder that, right now, the road for Indian equities runs straight through Washington and the fate of the trade deal.
