The market extended its winning run, and a key technical wall finally gave way. **On Monday, July 6, 2026, the Nifty 50 rose 159.50 points, or 0.66%, to close at 24,430.35, and the Sensex added 521.16 points, or 0.67%, to 78,285.07, a fourth straight day of gains led by realty and auto, with the Nifty reclaiming its 200-day moving average for the first time since late February.** A firm global backdrop and cheap oil kept buyers in control.

The standout was not a single stock but a signal. Ending above the 200-day average is the kind of technical milestone that pulls momentum traders off the sidelines, and it came with broad participation rather than a narrow, top-heavy push.

## What Happened

The rally broadened as it climbed. **Realty and auto stocks led the gains, and the buying spread well beyond large caps, with the Nifty MidCap up about 0.45% and the SmallCap index up about 0.75%.** After a bruising first half of the year, the market has strung together four consecutive up sessions to start July.

The backdrop stayed supportive. Crude oil held below $69 a barrel, a direct positive for oil-importing India, as tracked on our [crude oil price today](/blog/crude-oil-price-today) page. Global cues helped too, with Wall Street and Asian markets firm, keeping investors in a risk-on mood even as the rupee lingered near 95.4 to the dollar.

By the close, the Nifty had pushed above its 200-day moving average, the level it had been unable to hold since late February. The daily RSI moved above 60 and the MACD stayed positive, a set of technical signals that traders read as momentum building rather than fading.

## Why This Matters for Investors

The 200-day reclaim is the day's most telling move. **A close above the long-term average, after months below it, marks a shift in the medium-term trend and tends to draw trend-following money back in**, which can feed further gains. It is a sentiment turn as much as a technical one.

The broader message is that the recovery has legs beyond the heavyweights. Healthy breadth, with midcaps and smallcaps joining the advance, suggests the buying is not just a handful of index names but a wider return of risk appetite after a weak first half.

For long-term investors, a four-day rally that reclaims a key average is encouraging, but the real tests, earnings and the trade deadline, are still ahead. The tone into July is clearly positive, yet it leans on a friendly external backdrop that can change quickly.

## Market Reaction

Breadth was the standout feature, with realty and auto leading and the broader market participating rather than lagging. **Leadership from rate-sensitive realty and autos points to optimism about domestic demand and the interest-rate outlook**, a healthier mix than a rally driven by defensives alone.

Holding above the 200-day average into the close is the key technical takeaway. Traders will watch for follow-through in the coming sessions, since reclaiming the level matters far less if the index cannot stay above it.

## What Investors Should Watch

The first thing to watch is the Q1 FY27 earnings season. **It gets going in mid-July, with Infosys reporting on July 23 and other IT majors close behind, so their results and guidance will test whether the recent rebound is justified.**

The second is the July 24 India-US trade deadline. With the tariff clock ticking, any progress or setback in the talks will move the market, as covered in our [stock market in July 2026](/blog/stock-market-july-2026-what-to-watch) guide.

The third is oil and the rupee. The rally has leaned on cheap crude, so any rebound in oil would pressure both stocks and the currency, which is sitting near a record low, as tracked on our [rupee vs dollar today](/blog/rupee-dollar-today) page.

## Risks to Monitor

The clearest risk is a failure to hold the breakout. A quick slip back below the 200-day average would turn the bullish signal into a false start and could invite fresh selling.

A second risk is a weak IT earnings season. After the sector's recent rebound, disappointing guidance from the majors could quickly sour the mood.

Four green days and a reclaimed 200-day average are a confident start to July, but the month's defining events, earnings and the trade deal, will decide whether the Nifty can build on the breakout or slip back into the range that defined the first half of the year.
