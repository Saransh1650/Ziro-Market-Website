The IT relief rally has legs, at least for now. **The Nifty is extending gains near 24,180 and the Sensex near 77,500 as of July 17, 2026, led again by IT as Wipro shares jump in their first full session since results, though the Strait of Hormuz standoff keeping oil near $84 and the rupee near a record low still caps the upside.** It is a second cautious up day, not a breakout.

The market is leaning harder on a single sector. With Wipro rewarded for a steady guide and Infosys still to come, IT is doing the lifting while the oil-sensitive parts of the index stay heavy.

## What Is Happening

The market is being carried by IT for a second day. **After Wipro reported a soft but steady quarter with a flat guide, its shares jumped in today's session, pulling the Nifty IT index higher and the broader Nifty toward 24,180.** The breadth is still narrow, which is why the move reads as relief rather than a broad recovery.

Sector performance still shows the squeeze. Auto, metals, and financials remain under pressure from costly crude and the prospect of the RBI staying tight after June inflation topped 4%. IT is the exception, drawing a double benefit from Wipro's reassurance and a weak rupee that flatters exporters earning in dollars.

The macro backdrop has eased only slightly. Brent has slipped toward $84 and the rupee has steadied near 96.2, tracked across our [crude oil price today](/blog/crude-oil-price-today) and [rupee vs dollar today](/blog/rupee-dollar-today) pages, but with the Hormuz standoff unresolved, neither has moved enough to change the picture.

## Why This Matters for Investors

Earnings are back in the driver's seat alongside oil. **Wipro's rewarded guide shows that a result which reassures can move the market even against a heavy macro backdrop**, a sign the domestic story is not fully buried under the Gulf standoff.

The IT leadership is a useful signal. Its outperformance shows how a weak rupee redistributes winners and losers, cushioning exporters even as it hurts importers and consumers. That dynamic will keep mattering through earnings season, with Infosys on July 23 the next and biggest test, previewed in our [Infosys Q1 FY27 results](/blog/infosys-q1-fy27-results-preview-2026) piece.

For long-term investors, a firmer, narrow market is healthier than the crash that preceded it, but it still rests on an unresolved conflict. The direction from here depends heavily on whether the Hormuz standoff eases or escalates.

## What To Watch

The first thing to watch is the Strait of Hormuz and oil. **Because the market's caution rests on the standoff, any de-escalation could broaden the rally, while a confirmed closure would likely wipe out the IT-led gains.**

The second is Infosys earnings on July 23. As the only big IT firm to give full-year guidance, its outlook will confirm or complicate the stabilising picture Wipro just offered.

The third is the rupee and foreign flows. With the currency near a record low, any heavy foreign selling would pressure both stocks and the rupee further.

## Risks to Monitor

The clearest risk is an escalation over Hormuz. A confirmed, sustained closure would spike oil and hit Indian equities hard, IT tailwind or not.

A second risk is persistent inflation. With CPI above target and oil high, the RBI is likely to stay cautious, a headwind for rate-sensitive sectors.

The third is foreign outflows. Sustained selling by foreign investors as the rupee falls would weigh on the market. This is general information, not investment advice.

A Nifty near 24,180 on a second IT-led day is a market taking what support it can find. Wipro delivered, and Infosys is the next and larger test, but until the Strait of Hormuz question resolves, every up day is likely to stay narrow and provisional, one headline away from being undone.
