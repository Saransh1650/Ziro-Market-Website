Nothing went wrong with Indian earnings. The problem arrived by tanker, and then a second one arrived from Wyoming.

## Why did the market stall?

Because the oil import bill got bigger and the cost of global money went up in the same week. **Renewed US air strikes on Iranian targets near the Strait of Hormuz pushed Brent toward $97 a barrel in early September 2026**, and India imports more than 85% of the crude it uses, so this lands on the trade deficit, the rupee and inflation at once, a chain our [crude oil price today](/blog/crude-oil-price-today) page follows.

The second weight came from the bond market. **The US 30-year Treasury yield touched 5.337% earlier in the month, its highest in nearly two decades, before the Treasury announced larger buybacks to contain borrowing costs**, and elevated global yields make every emerging market look relatively less attractive.

Underneath the flat index there was real rotation. **Metal and realty stocks gained on the day while IT, FMCG, auto, media and pharma sold off**, which is what a market does when it is repricing input costs rather than growth.

## What is holding the market up?

Domestic flows and a decent earnings season. **Foreign portfolio investors bought Rs 16,621 crore of Indian equities in the first half of August 2026, a genuine reversal after roughly Rs 2.4 trillion of outflows earlier in the year**, though that buying rests on an assumption about softer US rates that this week could test.

Valuations are not the constraint at the index level either. The Nifty near 24,250 is not stretched by its own history, even if small and mid caps carry more froth, a split our [is the Indian market overvalued](/blog/indian-stock-market-overvalued-2026) analysis measures.

## What to watch next

**SEBI cleared the NSE IPO on 4 September, with the price band due around 11 September and subscription from about 15 September**, India's largest ever issue arriving mid-month, covered in our [NSE IPO](/blog/nse-ipo-drhp-2026-india-largest-ever) timeline and [NSE vs BSE](/blog/nse-vs-bse-ipo-valuation-2026) comparison.

**The 15 to 16 September Fed decision is the other main event, with roughly a 66% chance of a hike priced after Warsh's Jackson Hole speech**, a shift our [September Fed hike and India](/blog/fed-rate-hike-september-2026-india-impact) analysis works through. A hike would pressure the rupee and close the door on an RBI cut this year.

**Warsh already delivered the hawkish signal on 28 August, and September hike odds moved from about 35% to roughly 66%.** That puts August's Rs 16,621 crore of foreign buying at risk, as our [Jackson Hole 2026](/blog/jackson-hole-2026-warsh-india-impact) coverage sets out.

**Nvidia beat on 26 August, reporting $96.22 billion of revenue and guiding the current quarter to $108 billion**, which keeps the AI capex cycle intact and is the most direct global support Indian IT has right now.

**Then September gets busy domestically**, with the NSE IPO expected to open, SEBI's pre-open auction alignment on 7 September and the festive season starting, all covered in our [September 2026 outlook](/blog/stock-market-september-2026-what-to-watch).

## Risks to monitor

<Callout type="risk" title="The combination with no domestic offset">

Oil above $95 plus a US rate hike in September would weaken the rupee, keep inflation above the Reserve Bank's comfort zone, close the door on a rate cut this year and pull foreign money out, all while Indian company results stay perfectly healthy. That is how a good earnings year still produces a flat index.

</Callout>

The second risk runs the other way, and it is worth holding in mind before turning defensive. Iran's president signalled during the same week that Tehran wants the war to end soon, and a softer sanctions package with a dovish Warsh would remove two weights inside a week.

The third is the rupee itself at 95.76, which has little cushion left if oil stays here. This is general information, not investment advice.

For most of this year the Indian market has been described as resilient, and it has been. What last week showed is the shape of that resilience: earnings hold, domestic flows hold, and the index still cannot move until somebody in Washington decides what happens to a barrel of oil.
