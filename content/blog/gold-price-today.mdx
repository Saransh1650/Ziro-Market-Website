Gold gave back its gains this week. **Spot gold traded around $4,383 an ounce on 18 September 2026 and was near $4,300 on 25 September, after the US 10-year Treasury yield rose to 5.106% on 24 September, its highest since 2007.** A rising yield is the textbook headwind for a metal that pays no interest, and this week the textbook won.

That is the story. **When bond yields climb this fast, the yield penalty on gold grows faster than the fear premium**, even with the Gulf conflict unresolved and central banks still buying.

The fear has a new source as well as an old one. **The Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act was signed into law on 18 September 2026, authorising tariffs of up to 100% on the five largest buyers of Russian energy**, which adds a fresh layer of policy uncertainty on top of an unresolved Strait of Hormuz conflict.

## Why has gold held above $4,000?

Because three sources of demand are not rate sensitive. **Central banks bought about 863 tonnes of gold on a net basis in 2025, a fifteenth consecutive year of net purchases**, and that buying is a reserve policy decision rather than a yield trade, as our [central banks buying gold](/blog/central-banks-buying-gold-2026) piece explains.

Central banks added about 863 tonnes net in 2025, a fifteenth straight year of buying, and that demand does not respond to interest rates." credit="Photo: Ibex73 / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY 4.0" />

The second is the conflict premium. **The Gulf is still the swing factor: Brent spiked to $108.75 after Saudi Arabia shut its East-West pipeline in mid-September, and it ended this week near $105.7.** An oil-led inflation scare helps gold even while rates rise, which is the unusual combination being traded now.

The third is the policy risk that arrived on 18 September. **A law authorising tariffs of up to 100% on the biggest buyers of Russian energy is the kind of rule change that makes reserve managers prefer an asset nobody can freeze or tariff.**

## What is still capping the rally

The Federal Reserve and the bond market. **Every rate rise widens the gap between what a government bond pays and what gold pays, which is nothing**, and the September projections point to a policy rate around 4.1% through 2027.

That is why gold is 23% below its January record rather than testing it. The metal has recovered about 5% from July, not 30%.

## What it costs in India

The rupee changes the arithmetic for Indian buyers. **24K gold was quoted around Rs 1,52,840 per 10 grams on 25 September 2026, close to Rs 15,284 a gram**, well above where the dollar move alone would put it, because a rupee near 95.8 raises the cost of every imported gram.

That gap between the dollar price and the local price is the single most misunderstood thing about buying gold in India. Our [gold rate today in India](/blog/gold-rate-today-india) page tracks the city-level rates, [how to invest in gold in India](/blog/how-to-invest-in-gold-india) covers the formats worth using now that Sovereign Gold Bond issuance has ended, and [gold vs silver in 2026](/blog/gold-vs-silver-2026) covers the metal that has been moving faster.

## What to watch

**The first is the Federal Reserve's next meeting and the language around it**, because the move itself is usually priced while the signal about the path is not.

**The second is whether the Russian energy tariff authority is actually used within its 30-day window**, since a tariff applied to India or China is a genuine inflation event rather than a headline.

**The third is Indian festive demand, which runs from late September through Diwali** and is the seasonal support under local premiums, covered in our [gold this festive season](/blog/gold-festive-season-2026-should-you-buy) piece.

## Risks to monitor

<Callout type="risk" title="A resolved Gulf conflict is the fastest way down">

Gold is holding a 5% recovery built substantially on geopolitics while the rate backdrop works against it. A ceasefire or a reopening of normal traffic through the Strait of Hormuz would drain that premium quickly, and gold has already shown in 2026 that it can fall hard from a war-driven level.

</Callout>

The second risk is currency specific to Indian buyers. **A rupee recovery would cut local gold prices even if the dollar price held**, so an Indian investor is taking a currency position alongside a metal position whether they intend to or not.

The third is positioning. **A metal that rises into a rate hike is carrying crowded expectations**, and crowded trades unwind faster than they build. This is general information, not investment advice.

Gold at $4,300 with the 10-year at 5.1% is the fear trade losing ground to the rate trade for now, though not by enough to erase the year's recovery.
