Gold spent the summer going nowhere. It has stopped doing that. **Spot gold traded around $4,387 an ounce on 11 September 2026, roughly 7% above its late-July level of $4,085, though it is heading for a third straight weekly decline after falling nearly 2% on 10 September as US Treasury yields hit multi-year highs.**

The rally is not clean, because the other half of the gold equation turned against it at the same time. Markets are pricing roughly a 90% chance the US Federal Reserve raises rates by 25 basis points on 16 September 2026 after US CPI came in at 3.4% on 11 September, and rate hikes are usually gold's enemy.

## Why is gold rising again?

Two things changed since July, and both point the same way. **Renewed US strikes on Iranian targets have put a fear premium back into every risk asset, pushing [Brent crude above $100 a barrel](/blog/brent-crude-100-dollars-september-2026) on 11 September 2026, its highest since July.** Gold is the asset that reprices first when a conflict looks like it might interrupt oil.

Higher oil does a second favour to gold. It raises inflation expectations, and gold's oldest job is to hold value when money buys less. **An oil-led inflation scare tends to help gold even when interest rates are rising**, which is the unusual combination the market is trading right now.

What is missing this time is a weak dollar. The rally has come without one, which is why gold is recovering rather than breaking out.

## What is holding it back

The Federal Reserve. **Markets put the odds of a 25 basis point hike at the 16 September 2026 meeting at roughly 90% after the August CPI print of 3.4%, and every rate rise widens the gap between what a bond pays and what gold pays, which is nothing.**

That is the ceiling on this move. A metal with no yield competes badly against cash when cash is well paid, and it is why gold sits 22% below its January record rather than testing it.

## What it costs in India

The rupee changes the arithmetic for Indian buyers. **24K gold was quoted around Rs 1,55,510 per 10 grams on 11 September 2026, close to Rs 15,551 a gram**, far above where the dollar move alone would put it, because a weaker rupee raises the cost of every imported gram.

That gap between the dollar price and the local price is the single most misunderstood thing about buying gold in India. ## What to watch

**The 16 September Fed decision is the near-term swing factor**, and the language matters more than the move, because a hike already half expected is priced while a hawkish signal about the path is not.

**The second is whether the Iran escalation touches actual barrels rather than headlines**, since an interruption at the Strait of Hormuz is the scenario that has historically moved gold in hundreds of dollars rather than tens.

**The third is central bank buying, which set the floor under this entire cycle** at about 863 tonnes net in 2025, a fifteenth consecutive year of net purchases that continues regardless of what the dollar does this week.

## Risks to monitor

<Callout type="risk" title="A hawkish Fed can undo a war rally">

Gold is holding a 7% recovery on geopolitics while the rate backdrop works against it. If the Fed hikes on 16 September and signals more, the dollar strengthens and the yield gap widens, which has repeatedly been enough to pull gold back through levels the fear trade had just won.

</Callout>

The second risk runs the other way. A ceasefire or a de-escalation in the Gulf would drain the premium quickly, and gold has already shown in 2026 that it can fall hard from a war-driven level.

The third is currency specific to Indian buyers. **A rupee recovery would cut local gold prices even if the dollar price holds**, so an Indian investor is taking a currency position alongside a metal position whether they intend to or not. This is general information, not investment advice.

Gold at $4,387 is not the fear trade at full volume. It is the fear trade fighting a central bank, and the 16 September decision settles which one has been right.
