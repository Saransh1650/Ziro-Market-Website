## What Is Happening

Gold spent the first weeks of 2026 setting records, peaking near $5,602 an ounce on January 28 as Middle East conflict and record central bank buying drove a flight to safety. **Since then the metal has retraced a large part of that surge**, falling toward $4,040 as the macro backdrop shifted from fear to calm.

The biggest change is the end of the war. A US-Iran ceasefire reopened the Strait of Hormuz and sent crude oil tumbling, removing the geopolitical risk premium that had powered gold's climb. **When the world calms down, the fear trade unwinds, and gold is the purest fear trade there is.** Layered on top is the Federal Reserve. Under Chair Kevin Warsh, the central bank held rates but signalled it may need to raise them later in 2026, lifting the dollar and Treasury yields, a double blow for a metal that pays no income.

## Why This Matters

Gold's appeal rests on a simple trade-off: it offers safety and an inflation hedge, but no yield. **When fear fades and interest rates and the dollar rise, the opportunity cost of holding gold goes up**, and money rotates into bonds, cash, and risk assets. That is exactly the dynamic playing out as the war ends and the Fed signals higher-for-longer rates.

For Indian households and investors, gold is more than a trade. It is a savings habit, a wedding staple, and a currency hedge, since a weaker rupee lifts the local gold price even when the dollar price is flat. Gold crossing Rs 1 lakh per 10 grams in 2026 was a milestone, and even after the international pullback, Indian gold remains historically expensive. The structural story has not vanished. **Central banks bought about 863 tonnes of gold on a net basis in 2025, their 15th straight year of net buying**, a durable source of demand that does not depend on the day-to-day rate outlook. This is the foundation of the bull case that JPMorgan and others still back.

## What To Watch

The first thing to watch is whether the ceasefire holds. **Because much of the recent fall is the unwinding of a war premium, any fresh flare-up in the Middle East could send gold racing back up**, just as the conflict drove it to records.

The second is the Fed and the dollar. Gold is priced in dollars, so the currency's direction is a powerful near-term driver, and any softening in the Fed's hawkish stance would remove a major headwind for the metal.

The third is central bank buying. Quarterly data from the World Gold Council shows whether the structural demand that underpinned gold's rise is continuing. **A slowdown in official buying would be the most bearish structural signal**, while continued purchases support the long-term floor.

## Risks To Monitor

The clearest near-term risk is that the Fed actually raises rates. Confirmed hikes would strengthen the dollar further and could push gold lower, deepening the correction from its record.

A second risk is that the calm holds and safe-haven demand keeps fading. If geopolitics stays quiet, the fear premium that drove gold to $5,602 could keep unwinding.

The flip side is the upside risk for bears: any fresh geopolitical shock, a banking scare, or a dovish Fed pivot could send gold racing back toward its highs, as the metal has repeatedly shown it can move fast in both directions.

Gold at $4,040 sits between two powerful forces: a calmer world and a hawkish Fed pulling it down now, and a structural wall of central bank and safe-haven demand that has not gone away. Which one wins over the rest of 2026 is the question every gold investor is weighing.
