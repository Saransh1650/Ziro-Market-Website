The Indian earnings calendar has a familiar rhythm, and it always starts the same way. **TCS will open the Q1 FY27 results season in July 2026, and as in every quarter, the numbers will matter less than the guidance that comes with them.** With analysts braced for a muted April-to-June quarter, the real questions are whether global clients are loosening their budgets again and how fast artificial intelligence is changing what Indian IT gets paid to do.

Information technology is one of the heaviest sectors in the Nifty 50, and it reports before the banks and consumer giants. That makes the IT read a mood-setter for the entire season, which is why a quarter that looks dull on the surface still commands outsized attention.

## What Happened

The Q1 FY27 quarter covers April to June 2026, and TCS traditionally reports first, around the tenth of July, followed quickly by Infosys, HCLTech, Wipro, and Tech Mahindra. **The sector is expected to deliver soft sequential growth, with the spotlight firmly on the full-year FY27 outlook each company provides.**

Guidance expectations are modest. Analysts see the sector guiding for roughly 3 to 5 percent revenue growth in FY27. Infosys is expected to point to 3 to 6 percent growth with an operating margin of 20 to 22 percent, while HCLTech is seen guiding for 4 to 6 percent organic growth at a 17 to 18 percent margin. **These are cautious numbers that reflect a world where corporate clients are still slow to commit to large technology projects.**

Two forces shape the backdrop. The first is the uneven global economy, with US rates held high by the Fed under Kevin Warsh, a weak European economy, and lingering uncertainty from the 2026 tariff disputes, all of which make Western clients careful with their spending. The second is artificial intelligence, which has shifted from a buzzword to a genuine factor in how these companies win and price work.

## Why This Matters for Investors

The guidance is the prize because it tells investors how the next four quarters will look, not just the one that has passed. **A guidance upgrade would signal that the long-awaited recovery in client spending is finally arriving, while a downgrade would confirm that the demand drought is not over.** Given how much IT weighs in the index, that signal travels far beyond the sector.

The AI question sits underneath everything. In the near term, AI is a deal generator, and Indian firms have moved quickly to position themselves, with TCS striking partnerships to deliver AI services to enterprise clients. The harder question is the long-term one. **The traditional Indian IT model bills clients for large teams of engineers, and AI threatens to automate parts of that work**, which could shrink the revenue pool even as it lifts productivity. How management teams address this on their earnings calls will shape how the market values these stocks.

There is also a currency cushion. Indian IT earns abroad and reports in rupees, so the rupee trading around 86 to 88 per dollar in 2026 quietly supports reported revenue and margins. **A weak rupee can make a soft dollar quarter look respectable in rupee terms**, which is why margins may surprise on the upside even if demand stays subdued.

## Market Reaction

IT stocks have been rangebound through 2026 as investors wait for proof that the spending cycle is turning. As of mid-June, the broader market was being driven by the US-Iran ceasefire and the US Fed rather than by earnings expectations, leaving IT to trade on hopes and fears about the July guidance. **The sector is effectively in a holding pattern until the numbers land.**

When results do arrive, the reaction tends to be sharp and guidance-led. In recent quarters, IT stocks have swung several percent in a single session on guidance changes rather than on the reported profit, a reminder that the market is trading the future, not the past.

## What Investors Should Watch

The headline to watch is the FY27 revenue guidance from Infosys and HCLTech, the two companies that give explicit numbers. **A move to the upper end of the guided range would be the clearest sign that demand is recovering**, while a cut would weigh on the whole sector.

Deal wins, reported as total contract value, are the second signal. Large new deals take time to convert into revenue, so a strong order book today points to growth a few quarters out. Watch whether the deal pipeline is converting into signed contracts or stalling in client decision-making.

The third thing to track is what management says about AI, specifically whether they are winning AI-led deals large enough to offset any deflation in traditional work. **The companies that can show AI as a net revenue gain rather than a net threat will earn the market's trust.**

## Risks to Monitor

The clearest risk is that client budgets stay frozen, pushing the long-promised recovery further into FY27 and disappointing a market that has already waited several quarters. **A second straight year of low-single-digit growth would test investor patience with the sector.**

The AI deflation risk is the deeper, structural one. If clients start using AI to do in-house what they once outsourced, or demand sharp price cuts on AI-assisted work, the revenue model that built India's IT giants could face lasting pressure rather than a cyclical dip.

Currency is a two-way risk. A sudden strengthening of the rupee, perhaps from a calmer oil situation after an Iran ceasefire, would remove the margin cushion that is currently flattering earnings.

The numbers in July will be ordinary. The words around them will not be, because they will tell investors whether Indian IT is at the bottom of a cycle or the start of a slower new normal shaped by AI.
