Silver in India is sold by the kilogram, and the rate moves every trading day with international prices and the rupee. **As of September 3, 2026, the silver rate in India is about Rs 2,45,000 per kilogram, or roughly Rs 245 per gram, down roughly Rs 15,000 from the late-August peak**, as a hawkish Federal Reserve and rising rate-hike odds pulled precious metals lower together. It remains a favourite for both jewellery and investment, from coins to exchange-traded funds.

For buyers, the headline rate is only part of the cost. GST and making charges sit on top, so the final bill for silver jewellery, coins, or utensils is always higher than the quoted rate.

## Why the Rate Moves

The Indian silver rate is set by three big forces. **The international silver price, quoted in dollars per ounce, is the biggest driver**, and at about $63.87 an ounce (as of 2 September 2026) it has been falling as US rate-hike odds climbed toward 66%. When global silver rises, Indian rates follow within a day.

The second force is the rupee. Since India imports most of its silver, a weaker rupee makes silver more expensive in rupee terms even if the dollar price is flat, adding to the local rate when global prices rise, with the rupee near 95.76 to the dollar (as of 20 August 2026).

The third is silver's dual role. **Silver is both a precious metal and an industrial one, used heavily in solar panels and electronics**, so factory demand and the green-energy build-out influence its price alongside investment demand. That industrial link makes silver more volatile than gold.

## What It Means for Buyers

For jewellery and coin buyers, the key is to look past the headline rate. **A 3% GST and making charges mean your final cost is above the quoted silver price**, so comparing making charges between dealers matters. Coins and bars carry lower making charges than intricate jewellery or utensils.

For investors who want silver purely as an asset, silver ETFs on the NSE and BSE track the price without storage hassle or making charges. Given silver's volatility, it usually suits a smaller, higher-risk slice of a portfolio, and pairs naturally with a steadier gold holding. ## What To Watch

The first thing to watch is the international silver price, since it sets the direction for Indian rates. The Federal Reserve decides on 16 September 2026, with markets pricing roughly a 66% chance of a hike, which is the single biggest factor for silver right now.

The second is the rupee. **A sharp move in the rupee can swing Indian silver rates independently of global prices**, so currency news matters for silver buyers too, especially with the rupee near a three-week low.

The third is industrial demand. Because silver is a key solar and electronics input, the pace of the green-energy build-out is a structural driver that can support prices even when investment demand is weak.

Silver near Rs 2.45 lakh per kilogram has handed back most of its August rally, which is a better entry for festive-season buyers than they had two weeks ago. Where it goes next depends on the same forces, the dollar, the Fed, and industrial demand, that drive the metal. Knowing the live rate, the weight, and the charges on top is the difference between a good deal and an expensive one.
