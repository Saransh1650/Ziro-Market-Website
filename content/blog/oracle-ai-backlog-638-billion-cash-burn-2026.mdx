The demand question is settled. The funding question is not. **Oracle reported first quarter fiscal 2027 revenue of $19.3 billion on 10 September 2026, up 30% year on year, with cloud infrastructure revenue up 121% to $7.4 billion and remaining performance obligations at $664 billion, an increase of $209 billion in a year.**

The market could not decide what to do with it. **Oracle shares fell 5.38% to $152.94 in the regular session, then rose 4.34% to $159.58 after hours once the numbers landed.**

<Callout type="note" title="Where this stands, 20 September 2026">

The rate backdrop for long-duration growth has tightened. The Federal Reserve raised rates to 3.75% to 4.00% on 16 September 2026 and the US 10-year Treasury yield is at its highest since 2007, which raises the discount rate applied to AI capex that pays off years from now.

</Callout>

Both reactions are defensible, because the same report contains the largest order book in enterprise software history and a cash flow statement that is still deeply negative.

<KeyTakeaways>

- Q1 FY2027 revenue $19.3 billion, up 30%, with cloud infrastructure up 121% to $7.4 billion
- Remaining performance obligations of $664 billion, up $209 billion year on year, about half expected to convert within 36 months
- FY2027 guidance of at least $90 billion revenue, up 34%, and non-GAAP EPS of $8.10
- Fiscal 2026 free cash flow was negative $23.7 billion, with about $70 billion of net capex guided for FY2027

</KeyTakeaways>

## What the quarter actually delivered

The growth is concentrated in one line. **Cloud infrastructure revenue rose 121% to $7.4 billion, while cloud applications grew 10%**, taking total cloud revenue up 62% to $11.6 billion. The AI compute business is carrying the company, and the traditional software business is growing at its usual pace.

| Oracle, Q1 FY2027 | Figure |
| --- | --- |
| Total revenue | $19.3 bn, up 30% |
| Cloud infrastructure (IaaS) | $7.4 bn, up 121% |
| Total cloud (IaaS + SaaS) | $11.6 bn, up 62% |
| Cloud applications (SaaS) | Up 10% |
| Remaining performance obligations | $664 bn, up $209 bn year on year |
| FY2027 revenue guidance | At least $90 bn, up 34% |

**Guidance of at least $90 billion for FY2027 implies 34% growth for a company of Oracle's size**, which is the number that produced the after-hours rebound.

## The number underneath the number

A remaining performance obligation is contracted work not yet delivered. **It shows demand and visibility, and says nothing about timing, margin, or whether the customer can pay.**

<Logo src="/images/photos/data-center-server-room.jpg" alt="Server racks in a data centre, the capacity Oracle is building to deliver its contracted AI backlog" width={680} caption="Capacity has to exist before a backlog becomes revenue." credit="Photo: BalticServers.com / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0" />

The conversion schedule is the honest metric. **Management expects roughly half of the $664 billion to convert to sales over the next 36 months**, which is faster than the prior framing but still leaves the larger part of the book sitting beyond a three-year horizon.

<Pullquote cite="The test in one line">

A backlog is a promise about the future. Capital expenditure is a bill in the present.

</Pullquote>

Meeting those promises requires buildings and chips. **Oracle's fiscal 2026 free cash flow was negative $23.7 billion even after record operating cash flow of about $32 billion, capital expenditure hit $55.7 billion in the fourth quarter alone, and FY2027 net capex is guided near $70 billion.** That is a software company financing itself like an infrastructure developer.

## Why an Indian investor should care

The connection runs through two channels. **Indian IT services firms including TCS, Infosys and HCLTech sell to many of the same enterprises funding AI deployments, so the pace of AI infrastructure spending shows up in their deal pipelines with a two to three quarter lag.**

The second is physical. **India's own data centre build-out is part of the same global cycle**, and the capital, power and land decisions hyperscalers make set the pricing environment domestic developers work in, a link our [India data centre boom](/blog/india-data-center-boom-2026) piece covers alongside the power constraint in [AI's building spree](/blog/ai-data-center-capex-power-2026).

There is also sentiment. **AI capex results set the tone for global risk appetite, and Indian IT indices have moved on Nvidia and hyperscaler guidance more than once in 2026**, a pattern our [AI stocks India](/blog/ai-stocks-india-2026) piece traces.

## What investors should watch

**The first is conversion, quarter after quarter.** Cloud infrastructure at 121% growth is the backlog turning into revenue, and that rate holding through FY2027 is what would justify the spending. A single quarter proves capacity came online, not that the full book will.

**The second is the capex line.** About $70 billion of net capex in FY2027 against roughly $32 billion of operating cash flow means the gap is funded by debt and prepayments, and the cost of that debt is rising with US Treasury yields at multi-year highs.

**The third is customer concentration.** Very large AI contracts mean a small number of counterparties, and the credit quality of those counterparties is now part of Oracle's own risk profile.

**The fourth is what it says about the cycle.** Whether this is investment or overbuild is the question our [AI bubble 2026](/blog/ai-bubble-2026-will-it-burst) analysis works through, and results like these are the only real evidence anyone has.

## Risks to monitor

<Callout type="risk" title="Backlog quality is unobservable from outside">

The reported number tells you the size of the commitments and nothing about their durability. If AI demand cools, contracts can be renegotiated, delayed or cancelled long before they become revenue, while the data centres built to serve them stay on the balance sheet. That asymmetry is why a record backlog and a record cash burn can be the same story.

</Callout>

The second risk is the rate environment. **Funding a build of this size gets more expensive as yields rise, and US inflation data on 10 and 11 September pushed odds of a Federal Reserve hike on 16 September to roughly 90%**, as our [US CPI August 2026](/blog/us-cpi-august-2026-fed-hike-india) piece covers.

The third is timing. **Depreciation on tens of billions of dollars of infrastructure hits earnings well before the full revenue arrives**, compressing reported margins in exactly the period investors are watching most closely. This is general information, not investment advice.

Enterprise software spent thirty years being valued on how predictable it was. Oracle has spent one year turning itself into something closer to a construction company with a very long order book, and a share price that fell 5% and then rose 4% on the same set of numbers is a market that has not finished deciding how to value that.
