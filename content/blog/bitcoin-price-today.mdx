Crypto's stand-apart moment is over. **Bitcoin has slipped to about $62,850 as of July 9, 2026, down roughly 1.7% and falling for a second day, with Ethereum easing too, as the ongoing US-Iran conflict finally drags crypto into the broader risk-off mood.** Days after holding firm while stocks crashed, Bitcoin is now moving with the fear, not against it.

The shift is telling. Earlier in the week crypto traded on rate-cut hopes and ignored the geopolitics; now the geopolitics have won the tug-of-war.

## What Is Happening

Bitcoin has rejoined the crowd. **After holding above $63,000 even as Indian and global stocks crashed earlier in the week, Bitcoin has slipped to about $62,850**, sliding for a second session as the US-Iran conflict kept risk appetite subdued. Ethereum followed it lower.

The change is about which force is driving the market. For a few days, crypto traded on hopes that soft US jobs data would push the Federal Reserve toward rate cuts, and that optimism outweighed the geopolitical stress. **Now, with the conflict persisting and oil climbing above $79, caution has spread across all risk assets, and crypto has been pulled down with them.**

The reaction is a reminder that Bitcoin's independence from other markets is often temporary. It can decouple for a while on a specific narrative, but when fear becomes broad enough, the highest-beta corner of the market usually falls the hardest.

## Why This Matters

The end of the divergence matters for how investors read crypto. **When Bitcoin slips alongside stocks, it shows the market is now trading on the same risk-off signal rather than a crypto-specific story**, which ties its near-term direction to the geopolitics and the dollar rather than just the rate outlook.

The macro backdrop is the key driver. Crypto tends to do best when liquidity is loose and risk appetite is high, so a tense, risk-averse environment is a headwind. The rate-cut hopes that lifted Bitcoin earlier are still there, but they are being overshadowed by the Gulf conflict for now.

For Indian investors, the swings come with a tax sting. India taxes crypto gains at an effective 34%, with a 1% TDS on transactions and no loss offset against other income, one of the harshest regimes globally, as our [crypto tax India 2026](/blog/crypto-tax-india-2026) guide explains. That makes timing and risk management especially costly to get wrong.

## What To Watch

The first thing to watch is the geopolitics. **Because the slide is being driven by the US-Iran conflict, any de-escalation could quickly revive crypto, while further escalation would keep it under pressure.**

The second is US data and the Fed. The rate-cut hopes that lifted Bitcoin earlier are still in play, so a soft inflation or jobs print could reassert that tailwind even amid the geopolitical noise.

The third is whether Bitcoin holds $60,000. Staying above the level would keep the recent recovery intact, while a slip back below it would suggest the risk-off mood is deepening.

## Risks To Monitor

The clearest risk is a broad risk-off shock. A sharp escalation in the Gulf or a spike in the dollar could pull crypto down alongside other risk assets, as is already happening.

A second risk is that rate-cut hopes fade. If US data comes in stronger, the macro tailwind behind crypto could weaken just as the geopolitical headwind blows.

Bitcoin slipping to $62,850 as geopolitics bite shows how quickly crypto's story can change. A few days ago it was the market's brave outlier; now it is falling in line with everything else. In a world this tense, the next move for Bitcoin may depend less on the Fed than on the Strait of Hormuz.
