Crypto is marching to its own beat. **Bitcoin was trading around $63,600 on July 8, 2026, holding above $60,000 and building on last week's rebound, with Ethereum near $1,774, as hopes for US interest rate cuts kept the crypto market firm even while stocks fell on Middle East tension.** The divergence is the story: equities down on war, crypto up on rate bets.

The move remains all about macro. A softer US labour market has shifted the conversation toward rate cuts, and crypto, as the highest-beta corner of the market, keeps responding to that above almost anything else.

## What Is Happening

Bitcoin has turned a reclaim into a hold, and then a grind higher. **After jumping back above $60,000 earlier in the month, the price has climbed toward $63,600, defending the level and adding to the gain**, while Ethereum has firmed to around $1,774. The rally that began on a weak US jobs report has kept its footing.

The catalyst remains the US economy. Soft labour data revived hopes that the Federal Reserve will cut interest rates to support growth, and for crypto, which trades like a high-risk version of the stock market, the prospect of cheaper money is a powerful tailwind. **The clearest sign of that pull is crypto rising this week even as equities fell on the US-Iran escalation.**

That divergence is worth noting. Indian and global stocks dropped as oil jumped and risk sentiment soured, yet Bitcoin held its gains, a sign that the crypto market is currently trading on the rate outlook rather than the geopolitical headlines that are driving other assets.

## Why This Matters

Holding above $60,000 while stocks wobble is a meaningful signal. **When crypto shrugs off a risk-off move in equities, it suggests buyers are focused on the liquidity and rate picture rather than the day's fear**, at least for now. That can change fast if the geopolitical stress deepens.

The macro backdrop is the key driver. Crypto tends to do best when liquidity is loose and rates are falling, so any data that pushes the Fed toward cuts tends to lift Bitcoin. The market is now parsing every US data point for confirmation of that shift.

For Indian investors, the swings come with a tax sting. India taxes crypto gains at an effective 34%, with a 1% TDS on transactions and no loss offset against other income, one of the harshest regimes globally, as our [crypto tax India 2026](/blog/crypto-tax-india-2026) guide explains. That makes timing and risk management especially costly to get wrong.

## What To Watch

The first thing to watch is US data and the Fed. **Because this rally was built on rate-cut hopes, the next jobs and inflation reports, and the Fed's response, will decide whether the move extends or fades.**

The second is whether crypto keeps ignoring geopolitics. If the Middle East stress worsens into a broad risk-off wave, even Bitcoin could get dragged down, so the divergence is worth watching closely.

The third is whether ETF and institutional flows return. The 2025 rally was powered by inflows, so a pickup in buying from large funds would be an early sign that appetite is coming back in force.

## Risks To Monitor

The clearest risk is that the rate-cut hopes fade. If later data comes in stronger, or the Fed pushes back, the macro tailwind behind this move could reverse quickly.

A second risk is a broad risk-off shock. A sharp escalation in the Middle East or a spike in the dollar could finally pull crypto down alongside other risk assets.

The third is that the recovery stalls near resistance. Bitcoin has a long way back to its highs, and rallies during a broader downtrend can run out of steam. This is general information, not investment advice.

Bitcoin above $63,000 while stocks fall is the kind of divergence that grabs attention, but it rests entirely on the rate story holding. In a market this macro-driven, the next US data point, or a big enough geopolitical shock, could just as easily bring crypto back in line with everything else.
