Crypto is not trading on crypto news this week. **Bitcoin fell to a session low of about $76,300 on 15 September 2026, down roughly 2% in 24 hours, after the US Senate failed to advance the CLARITY Act, with more than 40 senators voting against the motion to proceed.**

The whole market moved together, which is the giveaway. **Global crypto market capitalisation fell to about $2.75 trillion earlier in the week, and Bitcoin is down close to 6% over seven days.** When everything falls by a similar amount, the cause is macro rather than any individual token.

## Why is crypto falling this week?

Because the calendar is hostile and stacked. **The US consumer price index lands on 11 September 2026, the Senate rejected the CLARITY Act cloture vote on 15 September, and the Federal Reserve raised rates by 25 basis points to 3.75% to 4.00% on 16 September after the 11 September CPI print.**

Rates are the heavier of the two macro items. **A non-yielding asset competes directly with cash and Treasuries, so every rise in the expected policy rate raises the opportunity cost of holding Bitcoin**, and the 10-year Treasury yield near 5.04% is the competition. The producer price index released on 10 September came in at 5.4% year on year with diesel up 24.1%, and CPI followed at 3.4% on 11 September, as our [US PPI](/blog/us-ppi-august-2026-diesel-fed-india) and [US CPI](/blog/us-cpi-august-2026-fed-hike-india) pieces cover.

The energy shock does the rest. **Crude near $104 a barrel keeps global risk appetite subdued, and crypto has traded as a risk asset rather than as a hedge through the entire 2026 conflict**, the same behaviour gold showed in our [why gold fell when the war restarted](/blog/why-gold-fell-war-restarted-2026) analysis.

## The vote that actually matters for crypto

The CLARITY Act is the one crypto-specific catalyst in the window. **It divides authority between the SEC and the CFTC, sets a test for when a token counts as a commodity rather than a security, and gives Bitcoin and Ethereum a statutory classification for the first time.**

The timing is what makes it binary. **The bill failed that vote on 15 September, with more than 40 senators opposed**, with attention moving to the midterm elections, which is why late August's push produced a 12% two-day rally in Bitcoin. Our [crypto decision week](/blog/crypto-decision-week-clarity-act-sec-september-2026) piece breaks down the sequence, including the SEC's 24-hour trading roundtable on 17 September.

## Where this sits in the cycle

Context stops the daily move from looking bigger than it is. **At about $76,800, Bitcoin is roughly 39% below its all-time high of $126,200 set in October 2025, but well above the $62,000 to $63,000 band it traded in during July 2026.**

Ethereum is the weaker leg. **At about $2,450 it has lagged Bitcoin through the recovery, keeping the ETH to BTC ratio near the lows that have defined this cycle**, a divergence our [Ethereum versus Bitcoin](/blog/ethereum-2026-vs-bitcoin) piece tracks, alongside the [Bitcoin dominance](/blog/bitcoin-dominance-altcoin-season-index-2026) reading that says the same thing from the other direction.

## What to watch

**Two of the three are now settled: US CPI came in at 3.4% on 11 September, and the CLARITY Act failed its Senate cloture vote on 15 September**, and the Federal Reserve then raised rates by 25 basis points to 3.75% to 4.00% on 16 September, as our [CLARITY Act vote](/blog/clarity-act-vote-failed-september-2026-crypto) and [Fed rate hike](/blog/fed-rate-hike-september-2026-india-impact) pieces cover.

**The second has already happened: the CLARITY vote failed on 15 September**, so the structural catalyst crypto was waiting on is gone for this session.

**The third is the Fed on 16 September**, where the decision matters less than the language about what comes after it, since crypto trades on the expected path rather than the current level. Our [Bitcoin H2 2026](/blog/bitcoin-h2-2026-range-breakout) scenarios set out the range levels that would confirm a break in either direction.

## Risks to monitor

<Callout type="risk" title="Three catalysts, one week">

A market with three binary events inside six days can move violently on any of them, and Indian investors carry an extra layer: a flat 30% tax plus cess on gains, no loss set-off, and 1% TDS on most transfers, which our [crypto tax in India](/blog/crypto-tax-india-2026) guide explains. Volatility with an asymmetric tax treatment is a different trade from volatility alone.

</Callout>

The second risk is a legislative stall. **If the CLARITY Act fails its procedural vote, the regulatory clarity that has underpinned institutional participation this year gets pushed past the midterms**, and the market loses its main structural catalyst.

The third is the reverse. **A dovish Fed surprise plus a successful cloture vote would remove two overhangs in 48 hours**, and a market that has spent the week de-risking would be positioned the wrong way. This is general information, not investment advice.

For a market that spent years insisting it moved on its own logic, crypto has spent this week waiting on a US inflation clerk, a Senate procedural clock and twelve people in a room in Washington.
