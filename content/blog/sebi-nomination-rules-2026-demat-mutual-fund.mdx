Every few years an Indian investing rule change gets flattened into a WhatsApp forward that says your account will be frozen. **SEBI's revised nomination framework took effect on 1 September 2026, and it does not freeze anything.** What it changes is what happens at the moment you open a new account.

**From 1 September 2026, any newly opened single-holder demat account or mutual fund folio must carry either a registered nomination or a signed declaration opting out of one**, and the field can no longer be left silent. That is the whole of the new obligation.

<KeyTakeaways>

- New single-holder demat accounts and mutual fund folios from 1 September 2026 need a nominee or a signed opt-out
- Existing accounts without a nominee are not frozen, they get SMS and email reminders twice a year
- Nomination stays optional for jointly held accounts and folios
- You can name up to three nominees, and a nominee is a trustee for heirs, not the owner

</KeyTakeaways>

## What actually changed, and for whom

The rule splits investors into three groups, and only one of them has to do anything today.

| Your situation | What the rule requires |
| --- | --- |
| Opening a new single-holder demat or MF folio | Nominee or signed opt-out, mandatory at opening |
| Existing single-holder account with no nominee | Nothing forced, SMS and email reminders twice a year |
| Jointly held account or folio | Nomination optional, changes need all holders' consent |

**For existing investors without a nominee, SEBI prescribed reminders rather than freezing**, which is the detail that got lost between the circular and the panic. Intermediaries send those reminders twice a year, and the account keeps trading and redeeming normally in the meantime.

The joint holding carve-out follows the same logic. **Nomination remains optional for jointly held accounts and folios, because a surviving joint holder already has a claim path that a single holder does not**, though any addition or change of nominee needs the consent of every joint holder.

## Why SEBI keeps returning to this

The regulator chose twice-yearly reminders over account freezes for existing investors without a nominee." credit="Photo: Jimmy vikas / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0" />

Because unclaimed assets keep piling up. India's retail investing base has expanded faster than its paperwork, with demat accounts crossing 231.5 million by June 2026, a number our [demat account boom](/blog/demat-accounts-231-million-june-2026) piece covered, and a large share of first-generation investors have never dealt with transmission of assets after a death.

**When there is no nominee, the family has to produce succession certificates, probate or legal heir documentation to claim the holdings, a process that runs into months and legal costs.** With a nominee on record, the intermediary can transmit the holdings on a far shorter document trail.

<Callout type="note" title="A nominee is not an heir">

Indian courts have consistently held that a nominee receives the assets as a trustee for the legal heirs. Nomination decides who the intermediary hands the holdings to, while succession law or a valid will decides who finally owns them. Naming a nominee and writing a will are two different jobs, and doing only one of them leaves the other unfinished.

</Callout>

## What to actually do about it

The practical answer for most people takes ten minutes. **You can name up to three nominees on a demat account or mutual fund folio, with the share for each one stated**, and where there are multiple nominees they can either continue in the same account after the holder's death or open separate accounts for their respective portions.

Splitting the shares explicitly is the part worth thinking about. **A single nominee on a portfolio with three heirs creates exactly the dispute the framework is meant to avoid**, since the nominee holds the assets for everyone but controls the account.

If you would rather not name anyone, that is a valid answer, provided it is recorded. **The opt-out is a formal declaration, not silence**, and once it is on file the reminders stop.

For anyone opening an account now, the choice arrives during onboarding anyway. The rest of the workflow is unchanged, and our [how to start investing in stocks in India](/blog/how-to-start-investing-in-stocks-india) guide walks through the account opening sequence, while [how to choose a mutual fund](/blog/how-to-choose-a-mutual-fund-india) covers the folio side.

One more thing decides how painless this is later. **Keep the nominee's date of birth, PAN and contact details current, because transmission is delayed far more often by a stale address or a wrong PAN than by a missing form.** A nominee the intermediary cannot reach is functionally the same problem the rule was written to solve.

The regulator's broader direction has been the same for two years: fewer unclaimed assets, cleaner transmission, and rules that nudge rather than punish, a pattern visible across the [SEBI mutual fund regulations](/blog/sebi-mutual-funds-regulations-2026) as well. Most of the anxiety around each change comes from the forwarded summary rather than the circular, and the circular is almost always the calmer document.
