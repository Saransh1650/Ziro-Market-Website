Six weeks ago the argument was about when the US Federal Reserve would cut. **On 16 September 2026 it raised the target range for the federal funds rate by 25 basis points to 3.75% to 4.00%, the first increase since July 2023**, and the projections published alongside pointed to about 4.1% at the end of this year and the same level through next year.

Chair Kevin Warsh framed it as a withdrawal rather than a squeeze. **"We removed a dose of accommodation so that financial and credit conditions would be more consistent with our ultimate objectives," he said**, adding that this summer's inflation readings did not tell him underlying trends had meaningfully improved.

None of that was decided in Mumbai, and all of it lands there.

## How the case was built

**Warsh delivered the shift.** His first Jackson Hole keynote as chair sharpened the inflation warning he had given after the July meeting and pledged a return to the 2% target, which analysts read as an endorsement of a September move rather than a defence of the hold.

His Fed raised rates on 16 September, the first US hike since July 2023." credit="Photo: The White House / Wikimedia Commons, Public domain" />

**The war supplied the reason.** Strikes around the Strait of Hormuz took Brent above $100 by 9 September 2026 and to about $107 by 15 September, and an energy shock is the kind of inflation a central bank finds hardest to look through after recommitting to a target. Our [Brent crosses $100](/blog/brent-crude-100-dollars-september-2026) note covers the India transmission.

**The July vote had already hinted at it.** The FOMC held at 3.50% to 3.75% on 29 July 2026 by 9 votes to 3, with Beth Hammack, Neel Kashkari and Lorie Logan dissenting in favour of a hike, the first time in a decade that three officials dissented in the same direction.

## What it does to India

| Channel | Mechanism | Where it shows up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Foreign flows | US assets pay more, emerging market premium shrinks | FPIs had just turned buyers with Rs 16,621 crore in early August |
| Rupee | Narrower rate differential, stronger dollar | Import costs, fuel, electronics |
| RBI policy | Easing into a widening gap pressures the currency | The 5.25% repo rate stays put |
| Equities | Higher global discount rate compresses valuations | Nifty at 23,914.45 on 2 September |

**The flow channel is the fastest.** Foreign portfolio investors pulled roughly Rs 2.4 trillion out of Indian equities across 2026 before buying Rs 16,621 crore in the first half of August, and that return was explicitly premised on softer US rates. A hike removes the premise.

**The currency channel is the one households feel.** A weaker rupee raises the cost of every import, and India's crude import dependence crossed 90% in FY26, so an oil shock and a rate shock hit the same line twice. The rupee has held up better than expected so far, supported by Reserve Bank of India dollar sales and the $136.37 billion drawn through its [FCNR swap window](/blog/rbi-fcnr-swap-136-billion-rupee-defence-2026) by 31 August, which is a defence with a cost rather than a free one.

**The policy channel is the most consequential for anyone with a loan.** The RBI held at 5.25% in August with a neutral stance that left a later cut open, but Indian retail inflation rose to 4.82% in August 2026, a seven-month high, and now has oil pushing it. Our [will the RBI cut rates again in 2026](/blog/will-rbi-cut-rates-2026) analysis lays out how narrow that path has become.

## What the decision actually settled, and what it did not

It settled the level. **The target range is now 3.75% to 4.00%, and the projections point to about 4.1% at the end of 2026 and the same level through 2027**, which reads as one more quarter-point move pencilled in rather than a cycle.

It did not settle the path. **Warsh withheld his own dot, as he did in June, and he has removed forward guidance from FOMC statements**, so the market gets less help than usual in deciding whether this was a one-off adjustment or the start of something longer. How to read the chart, and its limits, is covered in our [Fed dot plot](/blog/what-is-the-fed-dot-plot) explainer.

The reaction suggested markets read it hawkishly. **The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell more than 600 points, about 1.2%, the S&P 500 lost roughly 0.4%, and the 10-year Treasury yield sat at its highest level since 2007**, a level our [10-year at 5%](/blog/us-10-year-yield-5-percent-india-impact) piece works through.

## What happens next for India

**The rupee is the fastest channel, and it moved first.** It breached 96 to the dollar on 17 September 2026, the first time since 24 July, tracked on our [rupee versus dollar](/blog/rupee-dollar-today) page. Indian equities opened flat and traded either side of unchanged through the morning, which is a calmer response than the currency's.

**The Reserve Bank of India's room narrows further.** The repo rate is 5.25%, August CPI came in at 4.82%, and a wider rate differential makes easing harder to justify, as our [will the RBI cut rates](/blog/will-rbi-cut-rates-2026) analysis sets out. What that means for an individual borrower depends on the loan's benchmark and reset date rather than on the announcement, explained in our [repo rate and EMI](/blog/repo-rate-home-loan-emi-transmission-india) guide.

**Oil remains the variable that could change the whole picture.** Brent traded near $105.80 on 17 September, and a de-escalation that pulled crude back toward the $80s would ease the inflation impulse on both sides of the world, a swing our [crude oil price today](/blog/crude-oil-price-today) page tracks.

**For Indian investors, weekly FPI flow data is more informative than daily index moves.** Foreign selling is the mechanism through which a Washington decision becomes a Mumbai price, and it shows up in flows before it shows up in the Nifty.

## Risks to monitor

<Callout type="risk" title="The combination with no domestic offset">

An oil shock and a US rate hike arriving together is the one pairing India cannot cushion. Oil widens the trade deficit while higher US yields pull capital out, and the RBI cannot ease into that without risking the rupee. Domestic earnings can be perfectly healthy and the index still goes nowhere, which is roughly what has happened since mid-August.

</Callout>

The second risk is the opposite trade. Markets have moved a long way in ten days on a speech and a military strike, and a de-escalation in the Gulf plus one soft inflation print would unwind much of it just as fast.

The third is the Fed against the Treasury. The Treasury spent August expanding long-dated bond buybacks to hold yields down while the Fed now signals tightening, and those two policies pull in opposite directions. This is general information, not investment advice.

For most of 2026 Indian investors have been asked to watch domestic earnings, domestic inflation and a domestic central bank. The last two weeks have been a reminder that on the days it matters most, the Indian market is a price taker, and the price is set at a two-day meeting in Washington that no Indian gets a vote in.
