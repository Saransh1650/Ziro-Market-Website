The biggest test of the IT earnings season is still to come. **Infosys reports Q1 FY27 on July 23, 2026, and the number that matters is not its profit but its full-year guidance, since Infosys is the only major Indian IT firm that tells the market how much it expects to grow all year.** Whether it raises, holds, or cuts that range will set the tone for the whole sector.

The setup is more hopeful than it was a week ago. TCS opened steady and Wipro followed with a soft but reassuring quarter, so the question for Infosys is whether the largest player confirms that demand is bottoming or breaks the fragile calm.

## What To Expect

The quarter itself is likely to be unremarkable. **Analysts expect sequential constant-currency revenue growth of roughly flat to 1%, with rupee revenue near Rs 42,000 crore and net profit around Rs 6,900 crore**, a steady rather than exciting result. The weak rupee should flatter the rupee revenue line, as it did for Wipro, even where dollar growth stays soft.

Margins are expected to hold. **Infosys has guided for a full-year operating margin in the 20% to 22% band, and defending that range through the wage-hike quarter would signal disciplined cost control**, the same steadiness TCS showed with its 24% margin. Utilisation and a lower fresher-hiring bill are the levers that usually protect profitability in a slow quarter.

Deal bookings are the other number to size up. Large-deal total contract value shows how much future revenue Infosys is locking in, and a strong figure would suggest the pipeline is converting even while clients stay cautious on discretionary projects.

## Why This Matters for Investors

The guidance is the market's real focus. **Infosys entered FY27 guiding for 1% to 3% constant-currency revenue growth, and whether it raises the floor, holds the range, or trims it will be read as a verdict on demand for the entire sector.** A raise would extend the relief that TCS and Wipro began; a cut would undo it fast.

The read-across is what makes Infosys special. Because it is the only big Indian IT firm to give explicit full-year guidance, its number is treated as an industry barometer, so peers like TCS, HCLTech, and Wipro often move with it, as our [Wipro Q1 FY27 results](/blog/wipro-q1-fy27-results-preview-2026) coverage explains. That is why a single sentence in the guidance can move the Nifty IT index more than the quarter's revenue.

AI runs through the whole story. Investors want to hear that Infosys Topaz and its AI deal pipeline are winning fresh work rather than compressing prices, the same reassurance TCS offered in our [TCS Q1 FY27 results](/blog/tcs-q1-fy27-results-preview-2026) piece and Wipro echoed. Two steady reads make a third from the sector leader all the more important.

## Market Reaction

The market has already leaned in. **IT led the Nifty higher after Wipro's steady guide, lifting the index toward 24,120, so expectations into Infosys have crept up**, which raises the bar for a positive surprise, as our [Indian stock market today](/blog/indian-stock-market-today) wrap describes. A mere in-line result may not be enough to extend the rally.

Because Infosys also reports after market hours, its first reaction will show in its US-listed shares and then in Indian trading the next session. A guidance raise would likely spark a broad IT move, while a cut could drag the whole sector down with it.

The currency backdrop still helps at the margin. IT has drawn support from a rupee near a record low, since a stronger dollar lifts the rupee value of export earnings, as our [rupee vs dollar today](/blog/rupee-dollar-today) page tracks, one reason the sector has outperformed a market weighed down by oil.

## What Investors Should Watch

The first thing to watch is the guidance range. **A raise in the lower end signals demand is firming, a hold suggests caution, and a cut would revive the sector's gloom, so this single line carries the result.**

The second is margins against the 20% to 22% band. Holding the range through the wage quarter would confirm cost discipline, while a slip would raise questions about pricing pressure.

The third is deal bookings and AI commentary. Large-deal TCV and a credible AI revenue story would show the pipeline is converting, the swing factor across the sector as our [IT sector Q1 FY27 earnings preview](/blog/it-sector-q1-fy27-earnings-preview-2026) sets out.

## Risks to Monitor

The clearest risk is a guidance cut. If Infosys trims its FY27 range, the relief built by TCS and Wipro could unwind quickly and pull the Nifty IT index lower.

A second risk is the macro overhang. With the US-Iran war lifting oil and pinning the rupee near a record low, a risk-off market could overshadow even a solid guide.

The third is AI-driven pricing pressure. If management flags clients using AI gains to push rates down, margins and the growth story could both come under strain. This is general information, not investment advice.

For a sector that has spent 2026 trying to prove it can adapt to AI rather than be replaced by it, Infosys is the closing argument of the week. TCS and Wipro made the case that demand is steadying. On July 23, the largest of them gets to confirm it with a number no one else in Indian IT dares to publish, or to remind the market how quickly the mood can turn.
