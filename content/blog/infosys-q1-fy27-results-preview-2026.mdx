Indian IT got the confident finish it had been waiting for. **Infosys reported Q1 FY27 net profit of about Rs 7,000 crore on July 23, 2026, and raised its full-year revenue guidance to 2 to 4% in constant currency from 1 to 3%, the most bullish signal of the entire earnings season.** After TCS, Wipro and HCLTech steadied the mood, the sector's bellwether for guidance went a step further and lifted it.

This is the outcome the market had hoped for but not assumed. A raise, rather than a hold, turns a story of stabilisation into one of recovery, and it came from the one company whose full-year number the whole sector trades on.

## What Happened

The quarter itself was solid and in line. **Revenue near Rs 42,300 crore grew about 0.9% sequentially in constant currency, and net profit of about Rs 7,000 crore was up roughly 2% on the March quarter**, a dependable result helped by a weak rupee that lifted the rupee revenue line. The operating margin held near 21.2%, comfortably inside the company's 20 to 22% band.

The guidance was the headline, and it surprised to the upside. **Infosys raised its full-year FY27 revenue growth guidance to 2 to 4% in constant currency, up from 1 to 3%, saying client spending and deal conversion had improved.** In a season where the best the others managed was to hold their outlooks, a raise from Infosys stood out sharply.

The deal engine backed up the confidence. Large-deal total contract value came in around $2.8 billion, a healthy figure that shows the pipeline is converting into signed work, and attrition stayed comfortable near 13.5%, a sign the company is not straining to staff new projects.

## Why This Matters for Investors

The raise reframes the whole season. **Because Infosys is the only large Indian IT firm to give explicit full-year guidance, lifting its range is read as a signal that demand is improving across the sector, not just holding steady.** That turns three steady results plus one raise into a genuine recovery narrative rather than a mere floor.

The read-across is broadly positive. A more confident Infosys supports the case for TCS, HCLTech and Wipro, since its guidance is the closest thing the sector has to an industry-wide demand gauge, as our [IT Q1 FY27 scorecard](/blog/it-sector-q1-fy27-scorecard-2026) compares across all four. The Nifty IT index, down about 30% in the first half, now has a concrete reason to keep recovering.

AI remained central to the story. Infosys pointed to its Topaz platform and a growing pipeline of AI-led deals, arguing, as its peers did, that AI is winning new work rather than compressing prices, the reassurance first offered in our [TCS Q1 FY27 results](/blog/tcs-q1-fy27-results-preview-2026) coverage and echoed all season.

## Market Reaction

The first move came from abroad. **Infosys reported after Indian market hours, so its US-listed shares reacted first, jumping about 4 to 5% after hours on the guidance raise, with Indian shares set to rise on July 24.** A raise from the sector's guidance bellwether is exactly the kind of catalyst that lifts the whole index.

The timing amplified it. The result closed a heavy earnings week that also brought steady numbers from Reliance and HDFC Bank, broadening a market that had leaned on IT, as our [Indian stock market today](/blog/indian-stock-market-today) wrap describes. A confident Infosys gave the rally a firmer footing into the weekend.

The rupee angle helped at the margin, as it did all season. IT has drawn support from a currency near a record low, since a stronger dollar lifts the rupee value of export earnings, as our [rupee vs dollar today](/blog/rupee-dollar-today) page tracks.

## What Investors Should Watch

The first thing to watch is whether the raised guide holds. **A lifted outlook only helps if the coming quarters deliver against it, so the September numbers are the real test of the recovery Infosys just signalled.**

The second is whether peers follow. With Infosys sounding more confident, the market will watch whether TCS and HCLTech echo that tone in their commentary and future guidance.

The third is AI conversion. Bookings suggest AI is adding work, but investors will want the next few quarters to show that translating into sustained revenue growth, the swing factor for the whole sector.

## Risks to Monitor

The clearest risk is that the raise proves optimistic. If discretionary spending softens again, Infosys could be forced to walk the guidance back, which would hit sentiment harder than a cautious guide would have.

A second risk is the macro backdrop. With the US-Iran war keeping oil elevated and the rupee near a record low, a risk-off market could overshadow even a strong result.

The third is AI-driven pricing pressure. If clients use AI efficiency to push rates down, margins could tighten across the sector even as deal volumes hold. This is general information, not investment advice.

For a sector that spent 2026 trying to prove it could adapt to AI rather than be replaced by it, Infosys delivered the closing argument the bulls wanted. TCS, Wipro and HCLTech showed the floor was holding; Infosys, by raising its guidance, suggested the sector is ready to climb off it. The proof now lies in the quarters ahead, but the tone of Indian IT has shifted from bracing for the worst to daring to expect better.
