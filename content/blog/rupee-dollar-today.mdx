The rupee is catching its breath, and oil is doing the work. **The rupee rose 19 paise to close at 95.80 per US dollar on 25 September 2026, from 95.99 the day before, after an intraday low of 95.94, as hopes of a phased US-Iran deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz lifted risk appetite.** The Sensex regained 315 points to 73,895.74 and the dollar index eased to 101.10, though importer dollar demand and Brent near $105.5 capped the gain.

The month is still a round trip. **The currency rallied to a two-month high near 94.30 in early September before oil and US yields reversed it, breaching 96 intraday mid-month**, and it now sits about 1.2% above the record low of 96.96 set in May 2026.

## Why has the rupee been weak?

Oil is the first and larger reason. **India imports more than 90% of the crude it uses, so Brent running from about $92 in late August to $108.75 in mid-September, after Saudi Arabia shut its East-West pipeline, directly widened the dollar bill India must fund**, a transmission our [crude oil price today](/blog/crude-oil-price-today) page tracks. The pressure eased briefly when Brent touched an 11-day low near $101 on 21 September, then returned as it climbed back above $105.

The second is the dollar itself. **The Federal Reserve raised rates to 3.75% to 4.00% on 16 September 2026 after US CPI came in at 3.4%, and projected a policy rate near 4.1% by the end of the year**, as our [US CPI August 2026](/blog/us-cpi-august-2026-fed-hike-india) piece explains. Higher US rates raise the return on dollar assets and pull capital away from emerging markets.

The third is flows. **Foreign portfolio investors have pulled about Rs 2.45 lakh crore out of Indian equities in 2026**, and every rupee of that is converted into dollars on the way out, covered in our [FPI outflows](/blog/fpi-outflows-india-2026-record) analysis. They sold another Rs 5,027 crore on 24 September.

## How is the RBI defending it?

Not with rates, with reserves. **The Reserve Bank of India sold at least $8 billion in a single week in early September and has drawn $136.37 billion through its FCNR(B) swap window**, a mechanism our [FCNR swap](/blog/rbi-fcnr-swap-136-billion-rupee-defence-2026) piece explains.

The swap is also why the reserve number jumped. **Reserves rose by a record $44.9 billion in the week to 4 September to an all-time high of $785.7 billion as swap dollars arrived, then dipped $4.92 billion to $780.78 billion the following week**, the first fall after ten weekly increases. That firepower is why the rupee has fallen in an orderly way rather than gapping.

## What a weaker rupee does to you

| Who | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Importers, airlines, electronics buyers | Higher input and landed costs |
| Students and travellers abroad | Every dollar of fees or spending costs more |
| IT services, pharma and textile exporters | Dollar revenue converts into more rupees |
| Households | Imported inflation through fuel and goods |
| RBI | Less room to cut the 5.25% repo rate |

**The cruel part of an oil-driven depreciation is that it raises prices at exactly the moment the central bank would otherwise want to support growth.**

## What to watch

**The first is whether Brent falls back from $105.** Oil falling at source does more for the rupee than any amount of intervention, because it shrinks the dollar bill rather than financing it.

**The second is the US sanctions law signed on 18 September 2026**, which authorises tariffs of up to 100% on the largest buyers of Russian energy and names India among them, with a 30-day window to mid-October. A tariff would hit exports and the currency at the same time.

**The third is the Reserve Bank of India's 7 October policy decision**, after August CPI rose to 4.82%, which decides whether the repo rate stays at 5.25% for longer.

**The fourth is the record low at 96.96.** A break below that level would draw headlines and can accelerate corporate hedging, which in turn accelerates the move.

## Risks to monitor

<Callout type="risk" title="Reserves buy time, not direction">

Reserves near $781 billion let the RBI control the pace of a depreciation, not its direction. While US rates are rising, underlying dollar demand keeps building, and intervention spends reserves to slow something it cannot stop. Cheaper oil is the only thing that shrinks the bill itself.

</Callout>

The second risk is the feedback loop. **A weaker rupee raises imported inflation, which raises the cost of the RBI holding rates, which further discourages foreign inflows.**

The third is the upside case. **A signed Gulf de-escalation would reverse the oil premium, and the rupee's early-September high near 94.30 shows how quickly it recovers when the pressure lifts.** This is general information, not investment advice.

The rupee was not sold this month because of anything that happened in India. It is the price at which a country that imports nine barrels in ten settles its bills with the rest of the world, and a bill that briefly shrank on Monday and grew again by Thursday.
