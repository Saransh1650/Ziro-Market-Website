India's largest private sector bank delivered a solid set of numbers for the fourth quarter of FY2025-26, reassuring investors who had been watching whether HDFC Bank's post-merger integration with HDFC Limited was proceeding smoothly. **HDFC Bank posted standalone net profit of Rs 19,221 crore for Q4 FY26, a 9.1% rise year-on-year, with deposits growing 14.4%, gross advances up 12%, and the all-important NPA ratio improving meaningfully from 1.33% to 1.15%.** The board announced a final dividend of Rs 13 per share.

The results, announced on April 18, 2026, addressed the three concerns that had shadowed HDFC Bank's stock through much of FY26: the pace of deposit mobilisation relative to loan growth, the NPA trajectory in a challenging macro environment, and the bank's ability to sustain profit growth while absorbing the structural changes from the HDFC merger.

## What Happened

Q4 FY26 financial highlights for HDFC Bank:

**Net profit** of Rs 19,221 crore (up 9.1% year-on-year from Rs 17,616 crore).
**Net Interest Income** of Rs 33,081 crore (up 3.2% year-on-year).
**Total income** of Rs 89,808 crore.
**Return on Assets** of 1.96%; **Return on Equity** of 14.1%.
**Total deposits** of Rs 31.05 trillion (up 14.4% year-on-year).
**Gross advances** of Rs 29.60 trillion (up 12% year-on-year).
**Gross NPA ratio** of 1.15% (improved from 1.33% a year earlier).

**The deposit growth outpacing loan growth is a deliberate management choice.** Post the merger with HDFC Limited, which brought a large mortgage book but not a corresponding deposit franchise, HDFC Bank's credit-deposit ratio had been elevated. Excess reliance on wholesale funding and borrowings to fund loans creates interest cost pressure and funding stability risk. The bank's focus on deposit mobilisation over the past several quarters has been aimed at correcting this structural imbalance. Deposits growing 14.4% versus advances at 12% shows that gap closing.

NPI improvement from 1.33% to 1.15% reflects both genuine loan quality improvement and the bank's portfolio management. **Lower provisions in Q4 FY26 versus the previous year were the main driver of the profit beat**, as the bank released reserves it had built for potential stress that did not materialise at the expected rate.

## Why This Matters for Investors

**HDFC Bank is India's largest private sector bank by market capitalisation and an anchor stock in most domestic mutual fund and FPI portfolios.** Its quarterly results are closely watched as a proxy for the health of India's private sector banking system and corporate credit demand.

The 9.1% profit growth, while solid, is below the 15 to 20% annual profit growth HDFC Bank delivered through most of the decade before the HDFC merger. The merger fundamentally changed the bank's financial profile: a larger balance sheet with lower RoA and RoE in the transition period, which the market expected but did not fully price in during the euphoria of the 2023 merger completion.

**The improvement in NPA ratio is the most significant metric in the Q4 results for long-term investors.** India's banking system faced elevated stress in some segments, particularly microfinance and small-ticket personal loans, in 2025. If HDFC Bank's NPA remains contained at 1.15% through this cycle, it demonstrates the quality of the bank's underwriting standards and its ability to navigate credit stress better than peers.

RoE at 14.1% is the number management is working to improve back toward 17 to 18% over the next three to four years. The path runs through improving the net interest margin (earning more from each rupee lent), scaling down higher-cost borrowings as deposits grow, and managing credit costs tightly. **For investors, the trajectory of RoE is the single metric that best captures whether the HDFC merger is being successfully digested.**

## Market Reaction

**HDFC Bank shares reacted positively to the Q4 results**, with the stock gaining in early trading on April 18, 2026. The results cleared the bar investors had set: profit growth in high single digits, deposit growth strong, NPA improving, and no negative surprises on provisions or credit cost.

The dividend of Rs 13 per share, with a record date of June 19, 2026, provides additional near-term income for shareholders and signals management confidence in the bank's cash generation. HDFC Bank's dividend yield at recent prices is modest, but the dividend announcement after a clean quarter reinforces the positive sentiment.

In the context of FPI selling that has pressured India's financial sector in 2026, HDFC Bank's results being broadly positive reduced the risk of a further sharp de-rating in large-cap private bank stocks.

## What Investors Should Watch

**Margin trajectory is the key metric for Q1 FY27 results, due in July 2026.** HDFC Bank's Net Interest Margin (NIM) has been under pressure post-merger due to the lower-yielding mortgage book acquired from HDFC Limited. As the RBI cuts rates (repo now at 5.25%), the bank's funding cost should fall, but the lending rate also falls. Net NIM direction depends on whether HDFC Bank can defend lending spreads while benefiting from lower deposit costs.

CASA (Current Account Savings Account) ratio is the proxy for deposit quality. Higher CASA means more low-cost deposits. **Watch whether HDFC Bank's CASA ratio is improving with each quarter as the bank deepens its retail deposit franchise.** A rising CASA ratio improves NIM and RoE simultaneously.

The June 19, 2026 dividend record date is important for short-term traders looking to capture the Rs 13 dividend. The stock price typically adjusts downward by approximately the dividend amount on the ex-date, but short-term trading strategies around dividend capture carry timing risk.

## Risks to Monitor

**Credit quality in consumer lending remains a monitored sector.** India's unsecured personal loan and credit card book across the banking system saw higher delinquencies in 2025. HDFC Bank's overall NPA improvement is reassuring, but investors should watch the sub-segment disclosure on unsecured retail book quality in the detailed results filing.

HDFC Bank's mortgage portfolio, inherited from HDFC Limited, is long-duration. In a rising interest rate environment, the mark-to-market risk on the fixed-rate portion of this book creates accounting exposure. In the current falling rate environment, that risk is reversed, but the sensitivity of the mortgage book to rate cycles is something longer-term investors should monitor.

**Competition for deposits across India's banking system is intense.** Smaller banks have been offering higher savings account and fixed deposit rates to attract retail deposits. **If HDFC Bank needs to raise deposit rates to compete, its NIM compresses and profitability slows.** The degree to which HDFC Bank's brand allows it to attract deposits at below-market rates is a key competitive moat, and its preservation is a management priority.

HDFC Bank at Q4 FY26 is a bank in transition, still absorbing the largest banking merger India has seen while managing a challenging macro environment. The 9.1% profit growth, NPA improvement, and deposit focus in Q4 FY26 are evidence that the transition is proceeding. **Whether the bank returns to 16 to 18% RoE in three years is the question that will define its stock's performance through FY27 and FY28.**
