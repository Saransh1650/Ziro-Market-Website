Gold in India is sold by the gram, and the rate moves every trading day with global prices and the rupee. **As of 25 September 2026, the gold rate in India is about Rs 1,52,840 per 10 grams for 24 karat gold and around Rs 1,40,100 per 10 grams for 22 karat**, with international gold near $4,300 an ounce, lower than earlier in the week as US bond yields climbed.

The week is more interesting than the day. **The US 10-year yield hit 5.106% on 24 September, the highest since 2007, days after the Federal Reserve raised rates to 3.75% to 4.00%**, which is the textbook headwind for gold, even as the Gulf conflict keeps a fear premium in the price.

For buyers, the headline rate is only part of the cost. GST and making charges sit on top, so the final bill for jewellery is always higher than the quoted gold price.

## Why the rate moves

The Indian gold rate is set by three forces. **The international gold price, quoted in dollars per ounce, is the biggest driver, and at about $4,300 an ounce on 25 September 2026 it is roughly 23% below the record near $5,602 set in late January 2026.** When the global price moves, Indian rates follow within a day.

The second force is the rupee. **With the rupee at 95.80 to the dollar on 25 September 2026, currency weakness keeps Indian gold near its own highs even while the dollar price sits well below its record**, which is why the local rate rarely falls as far as the global headline suggests.

The third is duties and local demand. Import duty sits inside the price, and demand spikes during the festival and wedding seasons nudge premiums higher even when the metal itself has not moved.

## What it means for buyers

For jewellery buyers, the key is to look past the headline rate. **A 3% GST and making charges of anywhere from 8% to 25% mean your final cost is well above the quoted gold price**, so comparing making charges between jewellers matters as much as the gold rate itself. Coins and bars carry lower making charges than intricate designs.

## What to watch

The first thing to watch is the international gold price, since it sets the direction for Indian rates. **The Federal Reserve has already raised rates to 3.75% to 4.00% and projected about 4.1% through 2027**, so the next signal is the tone at its following meeting rather than another surprise.

The second is the rupee. **A sharp move in the rupee can swing Indian gold rates independently of global prices**, so currency news matters for gold buyers too, particularly with the rupee within half a rupee of its record low.

The third is the season. **Wedding and festival demand from late September lifts premiums and making charges rather than the metal rate**, so timing a large purchase outside peak demand is usually where the saving is.

Gold remains a cornerstone of Indian savings, and at about Rs 1.53 lakh per 10 grams it is close to its rupee highs even though the dollar price is a fifth below its record. Whether you are buying to wear or to invest, knowing the live rate, the carat, and the charges on top is the difference between a good deal and an expensive one.
